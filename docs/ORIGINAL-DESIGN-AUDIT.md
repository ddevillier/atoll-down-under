# Original-design and gameplay audit

6 September 2026. Gameplay baseline: `06e1f58`, the parked Windows testing snapshot. The accompanying revision adds fullscreen controls; it does not change the audited predator, food, health or scoring rules.

**Verdict: the remake has the broad structure of Odell Down Under, but its gameplay fidelity is incomplete. Predator pressure was reduced too far.** The problem is not literal absence of hunters: their speed, appetite, targeting and recovery rules make them ineffective or easy to miss. Passing survival tests was incorrectly treated as sufficient evidence of good balance. Survival, observation, meaningful danger and useful defenses need to be evaluated together.

## Evidence and confidence

- **Primary reference:** MECC's original [Windows User's Guide](https://www.myabandonware.com/download/m72w-odell-down-under), particularly printed pages 3–15. The manual establishes intended interactions, not undocumented timing constants.
- **Primary visual evidence:** original Windows captures of the [vertical wall](https://www.myabandonware.com/media/screenshots/o/odell-down-under-4ge/odell-down-under_7.jpg), [dense reef](https://www.myabandonware.com/media/screenshots/o/odell-down-under-4ge/odell-down-under_8.jpg), [wreck slope](https://www.myabandonware.com/media/screenshots/o/odell-down-under-4ge/odell-down-under_10.jpg) and [sandy terrace](https://www.myabandonware.com/media/screenshots/o/odell-down-under-4ge/odell-down-under_11.jpg). These were visually inspected against the current [nine-scene contact sheet](REEF-CONTACT-SHEET.jpg) and current gameplay captures.
- **Secondary reference:** [Lord Seth's Macintosh walkthrough](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/mac/927060-odell-down-under/faqs/42416). Its reports of specific species, exceptions and interactions are useful leads; platform differences and recollection require direct Windows confirmation before implementation.
- **Current implementation:** `src/simulation.js`, `data.js`, `feeding-sites.js`, `ground-motion.js`, `render.js`, `motion.js`, `main.js`, `learning.js`, `natural-history.js`, the desktop launcher, tests and prior validation reports.
- **Measurements:** 14,850 initial habitat populations; 24 controlled six-second predator encounters; 165 ten-minute exploration scenarios; scoring/protection probes. Reproducible data, per-species results and limitations are in [AUDIT-EVIDENCE.json](AUDIT-EVIDENCE.json). Population seeds are 1–30. Exploration seeds are 42, 7 and 91; seed 42 reuses the previous report under identical simulation rules, while 7 and 91 were run for this audit.

This audit did not execute the original Windows game, extract its rule tables, measure its animations or listen through its soundtrack. Consequently, it identifies confirmed implementation problems, documented design differences and unresolved historical details separately. A missing observation is not proof that an original feature did not exist.

## Why predators can feel absent

| Measurement | Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Initial populations: 55 species × 30 seeds × 9 habitats | Exactly two fish-eating hunters per habitat | Hunters are present, but population composition is highly uniform. A fish-eater is not necessarily large enough to eat the current player. |
| Species with at least one eligible hunter in the census | 49 of 55 | The other six are protected by size or a universal poison exclusion. |
| Of those 49, species without a faster eligible pursuer | 44 | Ordinary swimming outruns every sampled eligible hunter at its current pursuit speed. |
| Single hungry hunter, stationary unprotected player, no alternate prey or nearby shelter | 12 bites in 12 six-second fixtures | Contact attacks work when a stationary fish is exposed. |
| Same pairings, player swims directly away | Zero bites in 12 fixtures | Moving away defeats these encounters without an ability or shelter. This isolates one simple geometry, not every possible encounter. |
| Ten-minute exploration with regular feeding and cleaning | 165/165 survived | This routine needed no deliberate predator avoidance or defensive ability. |
| Exploration runs with no bites | 91/165 | More than half had no physical predator encounter at all. |
| Exploration runs with zero recorded chase seconds | 103/165 | Chases are very brief. This counter is rounded and sampled after a step; immediate bites can occur without recorded chase time. |

The exploration runs accumulated 117 bites across 27.5 simulated hours. They were not idle fish: the script visited habitats, selected meals when energy was low, and used cleaners. These results do not predict a child's survival rate. They show that ordinary routine play can largely avoid the need for the defensive systems.

The default Foxface example is especially revealing. Five of 30 starting Coral Cathedral populations contained no hunter eligible to eat it, despite containing two fish-eaters. Across all its sampled habitats there were 12 such locations out of 270. Every eligible pursuer was slower than the Foxface. The neon damselfish always had two eligible hunters in its sampled habitats, but all were slower than its ordinary swim.

### Causes, in order of importance

1. **Pursuit speed is globally reduced to 55%.** The player uses 100% of its catalog speed. A coral grouper pursues at 70.95 logical pixels/second while a neon damselfish swims at 132. Even the barracuda pursues at 112.75. The manual explicitly makes running, hiding and special abilities complementary choices; running alone should not almost always settle the encounter. See `Simulation.stepNpc()` versus `step()`.
2. **Several quiet periods stack.** Hunters start with 0–65 seconds of satiation. A meal or player bite grants another 45–80 seconds. An unsuccessful pursuit ends after six seconds and incurs 15 seconds of rest. Cleaner visits take priority too. Each may be reasonable separately; their combined pressure has not been calibrated against the original.
3. **Detection covers a small part of the widescreen reef.** The 165-pixel sensing radius is roughly 10% of the 1600-pixel water width. A whole unobstructed sensing circle covers only about 7% of the water area. Hunters choose the nearest suitable food, with many NPC animals and renewable shrimp patches competing with the player. That is less arbitrary than player-first targeting, but it can suppress encounters too effectively.
4. **Moving between habitats repeatedly encounters unelapsed delays.** Only the active section updates. A hunter's initial satiation in an unvisited habitat has not ticked down while the player explored elsewhere. A new habitat can therefore feel like another unusually quiet starting period, even though player protection is correctly removed on crossing.
5. **A bite is usually recoverable.** Every hunter deals the same 35 health damage, loses its target and digests. A well-fed player recovers injury at 0.4 health/second after ten seconds. Size has no effect on bite severity. That weakens the distinction between a nip from a small hunter and being swallowed by a much larger fish.
6. **Poison immunity is absolute.** NPC targeting excludes every poisonous player, with no predator-specific exception. Lionfish, devil scorpionfish and longhorn cowfish are consequently immune to predation. Giant grouper, tiger shark and great white shark also had no eligible hunters under the size/period rules. Some apex safety is reasonable; treating all these cases as equivalent is not.
7. **Tests favor survival but lack an opposing pressure requirement.** Existing tests correctly prevent repeated bites and unfair transitions. They also lock in a two-hunter ceiling and long satiation, without requiring hungry predators to threaten an exposed, eligible player during normal play. The prior 55/55 survival result could not establish that the balance was right.

### What should be retained

The current nine paintings are materially more varied than the earlier repeated layout. Mouse steering can interrupt a meal; edge travel does not teleport a pursuer onto the player; initial observation protection exists; nighttime remains readable; cleaning is based on waiting; diet hints and meal feedback are useful; saves preserve simulation state; the map offers optional directions without moving the fish. These are useful foundations. Restoring constant player-first attacks would discard progress without addressing the actual causes.

## Recommended gameplay repair sequence

1. **Record a small original-game comparison set.** Use silver sprat, a small reef grazer, a slow ambush fish, a defensive fish and a shark. Capture first entry, first meal, one pursuit, shelter, cleaning, poisoning and advancement. Measure time and relative screen distance, not guessed pixel constants. Resolve secondary-only discrepancies and create the roster ledger.
2. **Repair encounter mechanics before increasing populations.** Revisit the 55% chase multiplier, detection/engagement distance and predator-specific bite outcomes. Keep a safe entry state and fair boundary pursuit. A stationary exposed fish should be vulnerable; an active defense or reachable shelter should improve its odds; a predator should also visibly feed on NPC prey.
3. **Tune appetite, rest and habitat communities together.** Avoid fresh hidden grace periods on every new scene. Keep quiet observation intervals but permit memorable encounters in the same session. Change one group of parameters at a time and retain before/after data. Do not force a shark into every habitat.
4. **Restore food-chain exceptions and useful hazards.** Prioritize false cleaners, partial prey, hard-shell diets, poison exceptions and day/night relationships. Test the learning decision each creates.
5. **Repair scoring and progression.** Fix the inconsistent poison penalty, recover the Tournament ordering, and verify how food, cleanliness and energy should influence advancement. Distinguish intentional original repeated-feeding play from a new scoring bug.
6. **Then refine animation, readable depth and remaining guide content.** This preserves the user's gameplay-first priority.

Do not set a target death percentage from these simulations alone. Agree on acceptance through paired original/remake observation and a short child playtest: calm observation is available, predators are encountered, mistakes have understandable consequences, escape choices matter, and routine care does not require constant alarm. The automated suite should reject both unfair immediate death and an effectively consequence-free ecosystem.

## Fullscreen delivery and validation

The Windows application now starts in native fullscreen each launch. A narrow sandboxed preload bridge exposes only fullscreen state/toggle notifications; game code still has no Node or general IPC access. Visible controls keep their labels synchronized with F11. Browser preview uses the browser Fullscreen API.

The unit/asset suite passes 103 tests; the browser suite passes 27 scenarios, including fullscreen entry/exit through the title, dive and Settings, focus retention and compact top-bar fit. Native checks verify initial fullscreen, monitor coverage, leaving with F11, re-entering from the menu and preserving Escape inspection, along with prior gameplay checks. An actual desktop capture showed the reef filling the display; native bottom-edge hit tests at three points all resolved to the game window. The Computer Use screenshot backend was unavailable on this host, so desktop visual evidence used a read-only screen capture fallback. See `artifacts/fullscreen-desktop.png` and `fullscreen-bottom-hit-test.json`.

Only fullscreen and its supporting UI/documentation are changed in the accompanying build. The audit is a repair plan, not a claim that its open gameplay gaps have already been fixed.

See [ORIGINAL-ROSTER-LEDGER.md](ORIGINAL-ROSTER-LEDGER.md) and its JSON companion for all 64 current catalog comparisons, exact string IDs and source hashes. The executable has also been inventoried, but the original game has not yet been executed in this audit.

G09 is more concrete: sixteen current entries have no named counterpart identified in the original guide, and several comparison entries describe different species. Coral grouper, long-nosed butterflyfish, longfin spadefish and lyretail coral trout are specific mismatches. Original scientific spellings can also be obsolete or erroneous, so string differences alone are not sufficient to classify every mismatch. Full fact coverage remains useful, but does not prove original-species fidelity.

Behavior timing/spawning remains unverified. A targeted save migration applies the correction to saved lionfish while preserving custom diets, animals, positions and RNG. All 115 unit/asset checks pass, including approaching/eating a crab and exact saved-state comparison apart from the diet correction. ## Tournament preview follow-up

The Windows manual's printed page 6 explicitly describes a complete ordered list of playable creatures, with the first life highlighted and its information shown alongside. The remake previously abbreviated Tournament to seven names, an ellipsis and the final shark. The preview now exposes all 55 entries in a numbered, keyboard-scrollable list beside the first life's information. It uses the actual pending simulation sequence, so preview and play cannot independently reorder the roster. Challenge retains its four-life preview.

This repairs the missing preview behavior, not G08's historical ordering or G09's roster reconciliation. The manual's embedded screenshot is degraded; its text does not enumerate the full order. Current size-based ordering remains provisional. No difficulty, thresholds, species data or saved sequences changed. Verification: 109 unit/asset tests and a focused browser scenario at 1600×900 and 1024×768, checking all 55 unique entries, displayed/played order agreement, first-life identity, keyboard scrolling to the last entry and no horizontal overflow. Screenshots: `artifacts/tournament-preview-1600.png` and `tournament-preview-1024.png`. This source change is subsequent to the parked Windows population ZIP.

## Population follow-up

The initial audit underemphasized overall animal density. The baseline populated almost every scene with 24–25 NPC animals plus 20–26 food patches. The subsequent user direction requests population hills and valleys while keeping difficulty gentle. New habitat ranges and a modest second-hunter probability increase in Outer Blue now address that request; see [POPULATION-REVISION.md](POPULATION-REVISION.md). This improves G02 but does not establish original community or roster parity, resolve G01, or change the historical measurements above. Food-patch clutter, detailed local food chains and occlusion remain open.

## Scoring correctness follow-up: G06 resolved

Poison now reduces total score and current-life progress by up to 500 each, independently floored at zero, in scored modes. A low current-life score does not protect points earned in earlier lives; progress never becomes debt. Practice has no score penalty. The message reports actual total points lost, including a smaller loss near zero. Existing saves retain their recorded totals; prior poisoning history is insufficient to recalculate past progress. This repairs consistency within the authored scoring model, not historical formula parity. G05 and G07 remain open.

Regression checks cover poisoning just below Challenge/Tournament advancement, recovery of the threshold through a later valid meal, save/restore, floor behavior, Practice, created fish and valid specialist sponge feeding. Browser verification checks both displayed counters, visible and accessible penalty feedback, fatal-poison messaging and stage advancement.
