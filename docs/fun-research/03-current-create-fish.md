# Current Create-a-Fish: experimentation, identity, and life

Audit date: 2026-09-10. Scope: current source, tests, and executable simulation probes; no runtime changes or browser visual inspection. References below are repository-relative file:line locations. Proposals are explicitly distinguished from observed behavior. Older audit claims were not treated as current evidence.

## Assessment

Create-a-Fish already creates a functioning, named, recolored animal with consequential size, movement, metabolism, diet, period, and abilities. It participates in feeding, predators, shelter, cleaning, travel, observation, saving, and generic activity badges. This is a viable foundation for playful experiments.

Its largest limitation is what happens after creation: the player receives a scored survival dive, while the richer authored species lives are keyed to roster IDs and exclude `custom`. The fish has no persistent personal care record or experiment history. Several editor choices offer weak or unexplained tradeoffs, and a real shared-configuration defect can change a saved crushing fish's diet on reload. Prioritize trustworthy identity and small repeatable experiments over a sequence of replacement species or tournament-style score gates.

### Budget and ability quality

The default configuration costs **805 / 1,600**. All four attributes at maximum with no ability cost **1,480**; adding Burst swim still fits at **1,580**. Therefore “Every strength has a cost” is arithmetically true but most basic strengths can coexist at maximum. There is no need to balance speed, agility, and endurance until selecting a more expensive ability with a large body (`src/data.js:83`; `src/main.js:128,172`). This is a design assessment, not a claim that large size is universally optimal: large bodies still lose shelter and consume more energy.

Active powers share an 8-energy cost, 12-second cooldown, and require energy ≥12. Camouflage/lure last six seconds; other active powers last four (`src/simulation.js:203–214`). Their distinct names/prices exceed their mechanical differentiation:

- Ink, poison, spines, and puff all clear nearby targets and briefly stun/turn nearby animals. All four also receive the same defended-predator gate during activation (`src/simulation.js:213,390–394`). Poison additionally protects its eater from poisonous prey (`:218`); puff has visible inflation. They are not wholly identical, but much of their tactical outcome is shared.
- Shock, sting, and sonic all stun nearby animals for four seconds (`:213`); shock and sting additionally enter the defended gate, sonic does not. This makes shock's 250-point price versus sting's 180 difficult to justify from the traced simulation effect alone. Their generic visual ring is not evidence of a distinct gameplay effect.
- Lure targets eligible edible animals (`src/lure-motion.js:5–22`). A grazer has no eligible animal food at all: this selection spends 150 points and activation energy without an attraction opportunity. A size-30 hunter's fish threshold is 23.1, below the smallest roster fish; it cannot realize the “draw smaller fish closer” promise through ordinary fish prey. These combinations should be explained, made meaningfully useful, or prevented with a clear reason.

Food rewards are fixed at 500 for a food-object bite or 1,000 for an animal, even if energy is already full (`src/simulation.js:237–243`). Exposed survival adds eight points/second; hiding earns none (`:296`). Create mode has no score-driven advancement/win transition; that branch is only Challenge/Tournament (`:330`). Thus the main explicit reward favors accumulating meals and exposure rather than learning a sustainable routine. Care badges already provide a better alternative signal because they measure actual nourishment.

## Evidence: life, care, and persistence gaps

**The created animal misses authored player lives.** `ADVENTURES` is keyed by roster ID (`src/adventures.js:14–40`); ticking returns without a definition (`:126`). Feeding-school, ambush, grazing-route, and companion predicates explicitly whitelist roster IDs (`src/school-activity.js:2`; `src/ambush-activity.js:4`; `src/grazing-activity.js:3`; `src/companion-activity.js:4`). A fusilier-shaped custom fish has no school life; a grouper-shaped hunter has no authored ambush journey; a surgeonfish-shaped grazer has no renewing-garden journey. This is not evidence that all reef interaction is absent: generic feeding, cleaning, shelter, travel and resident observations remain available.

**Generic care works, personal recognition does not.** `src/journal.js:54–67` tracks needed feeding, healthy time, shelter, play, and school. However, journal species IDs come from `PRACTICE_SPECIES` (`:2–3`); `recordProgress` rejects `custom` for played/cared (`:37–44`). This is intentional and tested (`tests/journal.test.mjs:17`), and the UI honestly says creations can earn activity badges (`src/main.js:40`). Nevertheless “my fish was well cared for” has no lasting named stamp. Same-species school checks also cannot match naturally populated roster entities to `custom` (`src/journal.js:65`; `src/simulation.js:63`).

**Saving is real, but is not a creation collection.** Full world, custom config, player, observations, care and adventure state serialize (`src/simulation.js:473`). Portable import validates custom fields (`src/save-files.js:76–79`). UI saves on entry, every ten active seconds, return home, and unload (`src/main.js:144,180,253,281`); file import/export exists (`:197–207`). Do not claim creations are unsavable. However editor config initializes at page load (`:26`), and loading calls `resumeDive → enter` without hydrating editor config (`:144–145,197,207`). There is no blueprint library, duplicate/compare action, or saved experiment lineage in these paths. Death continuation resets score and respawns the same species into a repopulated world and fresh local care/adventure (`src/simulation.js:55–60,472`), so a continuing personal history is also absent.

**Confirmed shared-config defect.** `Simulation.start` stores the caller's object directly in `this.custom` (`src/simulation.js:48`), while UI passes mutable global `config` (`src/main.js:172`) and later edits that same object (`:218`). Creating fish A, returning home, and editing a new draft can change A's saved blueprint while its live species remains A. A direct probe created crushing grazer A, mutated the original config to hunter B, and observed live diet `[algae, grass, plankton, coral]` but reloaded diet `[fish, shrimp, worm, crab, snail, urchin]`: crushing migration recalculates from the now-mismatched config (`src/simulation.js:496`). This is an executable model reproduction; the precise browser route was traced, not browser-tested.

**Guide loses the custom context.** Opening the guide for custom explicitly selects `s1` (`src/main.js:181,249`), and food-web selector lists `SPECIES` (`:132–134`). The HUD diet is correct (`:88`), but the tool best suited to “who could eat my fish / what can my fish eat?” starts with a different animal. This is especially unhelpful after an unsuitable-meal message directs the player to compare diets.

## Ranked proposals and acceptance tests

1. **P0 — Preserve the fish the child actually made.** Clone configuration at start; distinguish immutable creation identity, draft configuration, and current dive state. Add “Make a variation” from a saved fish without overwriting the original. Acceptance: create named crushing grazer A, edit draft B to hunter, save/reload A; its name, diet, color, body, and world remain unchanged. Reloading a blueprint fills every editor control correctly. A/B drafts remain separate after page reload and export/import.

2. **P1 — Give this individual a small life worth returning to.** Offer a named care card and one short routine selected by compatible capabilities: grazer visits/revisits a patch; small fish chooses a refuge and returns after feeding; hunter makes a short feeding trip and recovers; broad-diet fish tries two appropriate food sources. Build on the existing care and journey systems without pretending the fictional fish is a real species. Acceptance: a newly created compatible fish can complete a routine in a few minutes; the named card persists after save/load; the routine remains repeatable with living world feedback; no species replacement or score threshold is required. Incompatible body/diet combinations receive an appropriate alternative, not an impossible checklist.

3. **P1 — Make choices testable before committing.** Add a small repeatable trial reef with three invitations: “Which food works?”, “Can I turn back to shelter?”, “How long until another meal?” Show a handful of predicted outcomes from the same rules as simulation. Keep the experiment playful: predict, try, observe, make one variation. Acceptance: at size 120 versus 125 shelter preview and actual shelter agree; changing only endurance reduces measured hunger under equal movement; changing only diet changes eligible meals; restarting a comparison uses the same conditions and does not consume the saved fish's progress.

4. **P1 — Remove hollow combinations and pricing.** Explain grazer+lure immediately; differentiate or simplify overlapping powers; show what each power actually affects. Explicitly say traits are game inventions/combinations. Acceptance: every offered ability has at least one reachable and visible beneficial interaction for every allowed diet/size combination; same-price/different-name options have observable differences; preview descriptions agree with range, duration, target eligibility, energy cost, and cooldown. Keep working passive Crushing jaws intact.

5. **P1 — Recognize care and curiosity in Create mode.** Make the default ongoing feedback needed meals, healthy recovery, observed relationships, and a named fish's milestones; retain score only as an optional experiment/challenge if wanted. Acceptance: full-energy food consumption cannot be the fastest path to a care reward; pausing does not advance care; a created fish can receive its own care memory without inflating real-species roster counts. A return visit shows the remembered fish and what the player discovered with it.

6. **P2 — Let anatomy communicate the experiment.** Separate freely chosen appearance from explained ecology, or visibly tie body/fin/mouth changes to abilities. Avoid silent template/habitat switches and unsupported tight-space promises. Acceptance: a single slider step cannot change starting habitat without preview explanation; a selected feeding adaptation has a readable matching action; color experimentation never implies stealth unless the model implements and explains it. Verify portraits and swimming at reduced motion and small display sizes.

7. **P2 — Put the created fish in the food web and notebook.** Add a fictional-creature entry computed from its actual stats and a short experiment record containing hypothesis, changed trait, and observation. Acceptance: opening the guide from a created fish selects that exact saved configuration; possible meals/predators match `edible` and `suitableMeal`; name/color and experiment notes survive export/import; no natural-history entry presents the invention as an actual species.

## Verification and limits

Executed `node --test tests/simulation.test.mjs tests/feeding-adaptations.test.mjs tests/save-files.test.mjs tests/journal.test.mjs`: **65 passed, 0 failed**. Direct Node probes verified three current templates, default/max costs, metabolism, inherited armor/poison flags, custom journal rejection, empty grazer lure opportunities, and configuration aliasing/reload diet corruption. Existing E2E coverage was read, not rerun: creation budget and selected fields reach the game (`tests/e2e/game.spec.js:636`); crushing diet preview (`:318–327`); portable custom save coverage (`tests/save-files.test.mjs:25`). Existing tests prove many mechanics work but do not establish fun, meaningful choice balance, personal attachment, or child comprehension. Those need focused play observation after the small experiment loop exists.
