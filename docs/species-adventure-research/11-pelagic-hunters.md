# Pelagic hunters: evidence and adventure decisions

Research date: 2026-10-10. Assigned roster: s17, s18, s28, s49. Research/planning only; no runtime or audio changes. Priorities: P1 = strongest next prototype, P2 = conditional follow-up, P3 = enrichment/backlog. Sizes: S = small existing-system addition, M = a bounded interaction, L = a new systemic feature.

## Existing implementation and novelty boundary

Read `src/data.js`, `src/natural-history.js`, `src/adventures.js`, `src/patrol-journeys.js`, `src/escort-journeys.js`, `src/school-activity.js`, `src/animal-profiles.js`, `src/cleaning-stations.js`, and the actual controls in `src/main.js`.

All four assigned species currently use the same patrol framework: E selects a start, a 360-unit lateral survey opens the feeding stage, a real suitable fish meal advances it, and a quiet three-second return completes it. s17, s28 and s49 additionally require a Space burst tied to the consumed prey's ID and incarnation. s18 has a high surface lane and does not require the burst. Those distinctions are parameters, not four different biological decisions.

Their exact current cards are s17 **A decisive pursuit**, s18 **Along the surface**, s28 **Speed with a purpose**, and s49 **A burst, then a breather**. The respective lane heights are 230, 105, 270 and 210. Data diets are fish; fish/shrimp; fish/shrimp; fish, respectively, but every patrol currently requires fish.

Important baseline: s17, s28 and s49 already have `social: school` in animal profiles. Only s0, s11, s42 and s58 qualify for the explicit plankton feeding-school adventure. Escort adventures already provide choose-neighbor, follow, leave for a meal and return. Ordinary cleaning already provides live clients, queues and C/Visit cleaner. Therefore school joining, escorted feeding, burst-return and a cleaner visit alone do not qualify as new adventures.

## s17 — a current-held school, not another swimming task

**Identity.** The roster and natural-history entry both name S. qenie. Museums Victoria's account identifies the dark bars and tail lobes and explicitly distinguishes S. jello and S. putnamae. Use that exact identity when considering behavior. [Fishes of Australia: Blackfin Barracuda](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/2554).

**Evidence.** That institutional page describes slow daytime schools at current-swept lagoon and outer reefs. WHOI independently describes a photographed S. qenie school hovering at a reef edge and maintaining position in strong flow. Neither establishes energy-saving wake positions, rotating leadership, cooperative prey corralling, or a required nightly separation. [WHOI: Celebrating World Oceans Day](https://www.whoi.edu/ocean-learning-hub/multimedia/celebrating-world-oceans-day/).

**Verdict.** Do not propose “join the current school, swim through it, catch something, return”: that is existing schooling plus patrol. A current-direction interaction would need broader water-flow simulation and evidence for an actual decision beyond position correction. Current station-holding can instead improve what the family sees: a few actual school members keep their bodies oriented as visible particles pass, with small corrections rather than permanent racing. This is ambient ecology, not an achievement or a timed hold.

**Bounded enrichment plan.** Reuse existing same-species school entities, with no quest-spawned ring or fake spectators. Add only a local authored current cue and reversible orientation response if a future shared current system exists. Do not introduce numeric energy discounts without further evidence. Pause freezes motion; leaving the habitat does not complete any discovery; copied entity IDs cannot impersonate a former member. A real-controls check is simply steering beside the group, pausing to inspect and resuming: schoolmates should visibly remain a school and no patrol or school-feeding completion should fire accidentally.

**Rejected transfer.** Great barracuda S. barracuda rubbing turtles and yellowmouth barracuda S. viridensis coordinated predation are not evidence for S. qenie. Their striking behaviors must not be silently borrowed.

## s18 — “Threads among the fronds”

**Identity and evidence.** The exact roster identity is T. crocodilus, also called crocodile longtom. Museums Victoria confirms its long toothed jaws, coastal surface habitat and mainly fish diet. [Fishes of Australia: Crocodile Longtom](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/2097). UWI's exact-species educational account describes eggs attached to waterborne objects by tendrils. [UWI species account, pp. 1–2](https://sta.uwi.edu/fst/lifesciences/sites/default/files/lifesciences/documents/ogatt/Tylosurus_crocodilus%20-%20Stickfish%20or%20Houndfish.pdf).

A primary study by V. B. Masurekar describes large filament-bearing eggs in seaweed masses and compares them with mature ovarian eggs and subsequently hatched larvae. That is a more distinctive basis than another surface hunt. [Masurekar, Eggs and developmental stages of Tylosurus crocodilus, Journal of the Marine Biological Association of India 9(1), 70–76](https://www.mbai.org.in/uploads/manuscripts/Article%205%20%2870-76%29640734450.pdf). The search service returned the paper's substantive first-page text and developmental passage, but direct PDF opening timed out: full-page verification remains an explicit pre-implementation check. A separate 1973 paper provides additional egg/larval identification work. [Bhatt and Bhargava, institutional repository record](https://nopr.niscpr.res.in/handle/123456789/39651). Its abstract was retrieved; direct opening was unsuccessful.

**Follow-up verification.** The complete [Fahay NAFO early-life-stage guide](https://www.nafo.int/Portals/0/PDFs/fahay/p776-823.pdf), printed pp.786–787 (PDF pp.11–12), was retrieved in this follow-up. Its T. crocodilus account explicitly describes attachment to vegetation, spherical eggs with long chorionic filaments and an egg illustration credited to Masurekar. This independently supports the attachment premise. The original Masurekar full-page/art check still needs retrieval; the guide does not establish active weaving, substrate-quality testing or parental guarding.

**Fresh decision.** Place a small reproductive deposit against visible suitable vegetation instead of treating the entire water column as interchangeable. This is an authored playable abstraction of attachment, not evidence that adults test weeds, weave nests, guard eggs, select maximum current shelter, or care for offspring.

**Four-beat candidate loop.**

1. Notice an already-present submerged seaweed frond and its fine attachment cue. The card explicitly frames this as a mature female's spawning episode; ordinary free-play fish are not silently assigned a sex or reproductive condition.
2. Steer alongside the frond. E previews the attachment position; a bare-water position says why it has no attachment surface. There is no invisible timer, season grind or damage punishment.
3. E once at the valid position releases one bounded egg cluster. Filaments visibly catch on the existing frond; the player is not shown tying them.
4. Swim clear. The cluster stays anchored while nearby particles move, completing the discovery immediately. No requirement to wait for hatching or return to defend it.

**Visible consequence and presentation.** The persistent cluster and its attachment are the ecological consequence; no extra adult mate, parent, helper or predator is invented. A tiny close-up inset can make filaments readable without filling the reef with giant eggs. The release is abstract and family-friendly. Do not hatch the eggs within seconds or imply a normal eight-second reproductive cycle.

The episode depicts one adult female's egg release, not a promise of fertilized eggs. Fertilization and later viability are outside this card. The visible release point belongs near the body's reproductive outlet; jaws do not carry or attach eggs. Attachment must use authored frond geometry separate from edible grass, so another animal's ordinary meal cannot create or erase a nesting surface by accident.

**Scope and hooks.** Add a separate bounded reproductive interaction, vegetation-anchor lookup and a small cluster renderer. Candidate state: `{reef, anchorId, anchorRevision, phase, clusterId, completed}`; phases are inspect/position/released/clear. At most one active cluster for this adventure and a small fixed number of rendered eggs/filaments. Completion depends on a real release onto a valid anchor, never elapsed distance or an old patrol meal. No new sex simulation is necessary if the episode is clearly labelled, but that framing is a product prerequisite.

**Persistence and failure.** Save the anchor-local position and released flag atomically so reload cannot duplicate clusters. Pause freezes release and strand motion. Offscreen time cannot finish the episode or hatch anything. A missing or replaced vegetation anchor resets the unreleased placement with a clear message; it must not attach to a recycled ID. Post-release visits restore the same cluster without replaying rewards. Leaving before release is cancelable without a penalty.

**Acceptance through actual controls.** Start from an ordinary s18 dive; use mouse steering and E to select and release; show that E in open water cannot deposit; pause with Escape before and after release; save with Ctrl+S and reload in both states; leave and revisit the reef. Verify one cluster, one journal result, no mating NPC spawn, no automatic hatch and no accidental Space/patrol credit. Reduced motion keeps the attachment readable without looping strand motion.

**Verdict / priority.** M / P2, conditional. The egg attachment is supported; the exact substrate-choice interaction is an explicit game abstraction. Before shipping, retrieve the primary paper fully and settle the mature-female episode framing. If that framing does not fit this game, retain the egg biology as an observation and do not disguise it as maternal caregiving.

**Rejected alternatives.** Leaping through the air would require a new surface boundary and is supported mostly as disturbance response. Ballistic feeding through Snell's window is documented in T. gavialoides, not this roster species. Slow drifting near prey is reported for T. crocodilus, but without richer decision evidence it remains a variation of hunting.

## s28 — “A passing brush”

**Identity.** The roster is E. bipinnulata; its museum entry supports pelagic reef habitat and an invertebrate-heavy diet. This cautions against defining every runner episode as catching fish. [Fishes of Australia: Rainbow Runner](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/4273).

**Direct behavioral evidence.** A 2022 primary study recorded rainbow runners scraping against both sharks and other rainbow runners. Conspecific approaches tended to come from behind and above, with a curve/rotation controlling contact; recipients usually changed course to avoid it. The paper treats parasite/irritant removal as a plausible function, not a demonstrated cure. [PLOS ONE: Sharks are the preferred scraping surface for large pelagic fishes, Results: Scrapee areas and Qualitative descriptions](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0275458).

The follow-up full-text check matters for presentation: rainbow-runner conspecific contact was mostly on the dorsal or lateral body, rather than the tuna-like rear tail edge, and evasive action occurred in 70% of observed conspecific scrapes. A deterministic recipient veer is an authored response for this short activity; it must not be described as an obligatory ritual, cooperation or consent. The evidence supports an evasive neighbor with agency, not a willing cleaning partner.

A 2007 observation also reports E. bipinnulata rubbing against a grey reef shark, followed by the shark turning and the group dispersing. [Papastamatiou, Meyer and Maragos, Sharks as cleaners for reef fish, author-uploaded primary paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/248160632_Sharks_as_cleaners_for_reef_fish). The grey reef shark is absent from this roster. Therefore the proposed episode uses the documented conspecific interaction; blacktip or whitetip reef sharks must not be substituted on the strength of a similar common name.

**Fresh decision.** Choose a clear approach to an actual moving schoolmate, execute one controlled brush, then respond to its changed course by giving it room. The point is a short contact-and-release relationship, not follow-neighbor, eat, return. No hunger, damage or parasite grind is needed to reveal it.

**Four-beat loop.**

1. Find a live, unoccupied s28 schoolmate. E selects that individual and reveals a short rear/upper approach cue; it does not enslave its normal movement or teleport it.
2. Steer into the cue. The local geometry changes with the schoolmate's course. If it is feeding, fleeing, cleaning or too close to another fish, the prompt describes the problem and offers another visible candidate.
3. Press E to attempt a single brush. A short curved pass and shallow roll show contact on a valid body surface. Turning away before contact cancels it; Space retains its ordinary burst role and is not a timing-test requirement.
4. The contacted fish visibly veers, as documented. Steer away into clear water; separation records the encounter. No second scrape is required and the selected fish is immediately free to continue its routine.

**NPC/ecological consequence.** The recipient alters its course, rather than being a passive progress token. A small temporary “rubbed here” visual can show the contacted body area. Do not show a parasite definitely falling away or award a guaranteed health cure: the biological purpose is still an inference. Journal copy can say “Rainbow runners have been observed brushing against one another; researchers suggest scraping may help remove irritants.”

**Bounded implementation.** One target identity `{reef, id, incarnation}`, phase approach/contact/separate, normalized contact position, a contact-committed boolean and a short animation progress value suffice. The target's evasive steering is bounded and yields to real danger/feeding/cleaning. Reuse escort identity resolution and body-frame geometry, but keep distinct completion semantics and no auto-follow for the entire episode. No continuous collision damage, ragdoll, schoolwide choreography or new NPC species is needed.

**Persistence and interruptions.** Save contact-committed atomically to prevent reloading from awarding multiple discoveries. Pause freezes both participants and cannot supply contact or separation. Offscreen progress is disabled. If the host dies, leaves, becomes unavailable or changes incarnation, cancel contact and show another actual candidate; never silently relink the saved host. A committed contact can retain the factual observation but the separation step must still occur on-screen if the host exists. Disappearance must not be treated as deliberate separation. Leaving the habitat offers an explicit canceled encounter rather than an indefinite hidden wait.

**Real-controls acceptance.** From normal s28 play, select with E, steer from behind/above and trigger once. Observe the NPC veer and steer away. Repeat with a busy fish, a wrong species, a canceled approach, a host removed/replaced, and no suitable schoolmate. Escape/P pause, Ctrl+S reload before contact and after contact, and habitat switching must not manufacture completion. A frontal collision alone cannot complete the episode. Reduced-motion mode replaces the roll with a brief pose cue. Journal completion is one real contact plus voluntary clear separation, with no fish meal and no Space burst requirement.

**Verdict / priority.** M / P1. Strongest proposal in this group: directly observed exact-species behavior, visible NPC consequence, existing roster compatibility, a short new decision, and no timer grind. The biological uncertainty concerns function, so keep the payoff observational rather than therapeutic.

## s49 — dense school and cleaner visit: enrichment only

**Identity.** Both code and museum source identify G. bilineatus. Its divided lateral line and unspotted belly distinguish it from G. bicarinatus. Shared “shark mackerel” common names are a substantial source-transfer trap. [Fishes of Australia: Scad Mackerel](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/722).

**New evidence.** The same institutional page describes dense schools near outer reef walls/deep clear slopes, a small-fish/crustacean diet, and captions an identified G. bilineatus at a cleaning station. The caption supports occurrence, not a detailed pose ritual or cooperative hunting strategy. Its bibliography includes Auster's 2008 predatory-strategy paper on G. bicarinatus; that paper does not validate those strategies for G. bilineatus.

**Primary cross-check.** [Collette & Gillis 1992, Fishery Bulletin 90:13–53](https://repository.si.edu/bitstreams/5316ebac-2f81-47a0-b988-bb2d5074bce7/download), retrieved in full, distinguishes the two taxa and summarizes large schools in shallow reef water, reproduction reports and known parasites for G. bilineatus (printed pp.44–45). It explicitly leaves the additional lateral line's function unknown (p.14). That anatomy is a useful inspect/art detail; it does not justify a second lateral line granting special tracking, current detection or a sensory superpower. A reported regional spawning season is not a demonstrated courtship/placement behavior suitable for a new card.

**Verdict.** Reject a new “leave school, visit cleaner, return” card: it combines already-present school, escort and cleaning primitives without a distinctive new decision. Reject “surround prey with schoolmates” unless new exact-species evidence establishes it. A school-density presentation pass and a supported crustacean menu would improve biological fidelity, but they are not novel adventures.

**Useful small work.** The natural-history limitation says there is no dedicated schooling routine while `animal-profiles.js` already marks s49 as schooling. Distinguish general social swimming from the absence of a dedicated feeding-school adventure when updating that copy. If cleaner visiting is highlighted, use an existing cleaner and real parasite need, C/Visit cleaner and existing queues; no invented hunger/parasites to force an episode. Preserve the existing pause/save/identity checks and ensure a normal service cannot accidentally complete the patrol. S / P3, with no new achievement recommended.
