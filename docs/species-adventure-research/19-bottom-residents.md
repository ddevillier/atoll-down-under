# Bottom residents: autonomous lives worth observing

Research and planning only, updated 10 October 2026. Covers **s46 Reef crab, s47 Red sea star, s61 Spiral snail, s62 Needle-spined urchin**. All remain non-playable residents. Proposed controls, habitat edits and contact mechanics below are unimplemented design work; this update changes only this report.

## What the game already does

Inspected src/data.js, resident-life.js, resident-journeys.js, resident-motion.js, ground-motion.js, and relevant simulation/render dispatch. The current names identify broad groups, not exact species: Brachyura, Asteroidea, Gastropoda and Echinoidea respectively.

| ID | Current autonomous life and observation | Difference a new card must deliver |
|---|---|---|
| s46 | Detritus contact/handling; jointed leg/claw deformation; authored close-passer withdrawal; committed meal followed by 24 units of ground travel. | A real host-care consequence after explicit reidentification, or a verified locomotion observation; no generic coral-crab upgrade. |
| s47 | Detritus contact/handling followed by three quiet seconds. Despite catalog speed zero, actual ground speed is 1.2 units/sec before satiation reduction. | Observable support contacts and their relationship to body movement, independent of feeding. |
| s61 | Algae contact/handling, foot/feelers deformation, authored withdrawal; meal followed by 18 units of ground travel. Ground speed is 2.45 units/sec before satiation reduction. | Spatially consumed food that changes the resident's next reachable route. |
| s62 | Night-only population; algae meal followed by three quiet seconds. Despite catalog speed zero, actual ground speed is 0.45 units/sec before satiation reduction. | A visible, persistent cropped boundary; no claim that crawling is newly introduced. |

residentStep already commits one 12-unit resource decrement during preparation/handling and starts 60 seconds of satiation. Uniform patch depletion/regrowth is not a spatial grazing model. walkOnGround follows and reflects along a polyline; decorative limb motion does not establish independent planted contacts. The guide's stationary sea-star/fixed-urchin wording is stale and should be corrected during a later implementation.

Existing entry is Esc → inspect an existing resident → Resume watching. Observation binds reef + entity ID + incarnation, and credits only that selected resident in the current reef. Extend this entry with explicit observation kinds; retain its earlier discovery and save compatibility. A player prediction, viewing position or resource decision may change what is learned or available, but never turns the resident into a controllable fish.

## s61 — Leave a little green

**Source → identity gate.** FAO describes *Trochus niloticus* as a radular grazer of turf/fleshy algae associated with reef substrate, including algae-covered dead coral slabs; its account also reports mixed material in gut contents. The accepted name is *Rochia nilotica*. This supports a named reef-surface grazer, not an algae-only diet for all snails. Require a verified topshell shell/foot, adult habitat and identification before changing s61's species claims. Do not borrow the separate *Turbo marmoratus* account's behavior. [FAO, section 2.2](https://www.fao.org/fishery/docs/CDrom/aquaculture/a0845t/volume2/docrep/field/009/ag150e/AG150E03.htm); [WoRMS/MolluscaBase naming record](https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?id=1251282&p=taxdetails).

**Player choice → actual consequence.** Inspect an already-foraging resident and two connected strips of an existing algae surface. An algae-eating player chooses which strip to take an ordinary physical bite from, or leaves both and eats elsewhere. The snail independently selects reachable remaining food, rasps a narrower band, and follows that band rather than an unchanged line. Fish bite and snail scrape leave different persistent marks. “Sharing” describes this observed resource arrangement; it does not claim snail gratitude or a social pact. A non-grazer can compare the changing strip without receiving an invented grazing control.

**Code / art / bounded state.** Extend residentStep, ground-food projection and normal player eating with one coverage source of truth: at most two connected surfaces of 12 cells each, cell commitment, one target cell and saved coverage. Patch amount must agree with cell depletion. Reuse shell/foot articulation after identity review; add contact-bound scrape geometry and a reachable-cell route. Any magnified radula is labeled as an explanatory diagram. Observation stores existing identity, chosen strip, before/after sample and actual actor-contact evidence; no mouse trail or accumulated particles.

**Acceptance.** Start through inspection and real swim/eat controls. Opposite-strip bites must leave different usable cover and change the snail's subsequent route; a remote strip click consumes nothing. Save during a committed scrape, reload, and verify no duplicate consumption or regrown cells. Leaving the reef preserves the changed surface without unseen witness credit. An exhausted surface gives a neutral alternate-patch outcome. Admission offers a currently foraging opportunity or a concrete alternative; it never resets hunger or asks for a mandatory 60-second wait.

**Rank: 1 — P0 / M, identity-gated.** Strongest first resident extension because bounded substrate state supplies both visible consequence and a meaningful resource choice.

## s47 — Which feet are holding?

**Source → evidence boundary.** Group-level tube-foot locomotion is supported by NOAA. Comparative research across 14 sea-star species found differing tube-foot morphologies/functions, cautioning against one exact mechanism for every star. Primary work on *Asterias rubens* directly visualizes attachment/release and links contact duration to crawling mechanics. Its experimental load/inverted-posture findings are not demonstrated for an unidentified tropical red star. [NOAA, sea-star overview](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/starfish.html); [Santos et al., comparative tube feet](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15549719/); [Deridoux et al., tube-foot dynamics](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2509681123).

**Identity gate.** Keep s47's name generic and its explanatory inset explicitly illustrative for a small enrichment. A full species-specific attachment adventure requires a named tropical representative, verified underside/arm art and appropriate locomotion evidence. *Fromia milleporella* is a possible regional/art candidate, not an inference from a red sprite and not a recipient of *Asterias*' measured gait or diet. Its museum inventory record establishes occurrence, not those behaviors. [Sampey and Marsh, Kimberley inventory](https://museum.wa.gov.au/sites/default/files/SuppWAMuseum_2015_84_207to246_SAMPEYetal_0.pdf).

**Player choice → actual consequence.** Inspect an existing star during ordinary travel. Choose a view of the near arm or body, then mark which visible contact you think will remain attached while the body advances. The prediction changes the comparison lens only. The star's autonomous support/contact sequence continues; the body shifts relative to planted feet, and released feet reach new substrate. Move the lens to compare the prediction with actual anchor and body positions. Either result yields a readable explanation and another optional prediction, without a right-answer streak or meal requirement.

**Code / art / bounded state.** The small version reveals honest existing ground displacement with a clearly diagrammatic inset. The stronger version needs real support anchors governing displacement, not feet flashing beneath a sliding sprite: a bounded set of representative contacts, stance/reach phases, contact IDs and one sampled body position. Extend resident contact pose and renderer separately from free-swimming geometry. Save selected view, one prediction, sampled contact/body positions and witnessed transition; the renderer never awards progress. Do not simulate hundreds of feet or create permanent “footprints” the evidence does not support.

**Acceptance.** Esc/inspect/Resume exposes the lens without steering or eating input leakage. In the authoritative version, a contact must hold its world position while the body physically moves, then release; pausing freezes both. Completion needs an observed contact/body relationship, not elapsed quiet time or a distance quota. Reload resumes the same comparison; a replaced incarnation cannot inherit it. Never flip, injure, weigh down or provoke the star to reproduce laboratory manipulations.

**Rank: 2 — P1 / S–M observation enrichment; L and evidence-gated for authoritative contact physics.** Better autonomous-life observation now; defer a full species-specific adventure until identity and mechanical evidence align. Reject coral predation, shell-prying, sponge diets and regeneration-care stories for this unidentified resident.

## s62 — The edge of a night garden

**Source → identity gate.** *Diadema setosum* is a plausible named long-spined representative: the WA Museum identifies its five white spots, red anal ring and mostly nighttime grazing. Require those verified markings and a documented reef habitat before claiming the sprite is this species. A primary cage study in central Japan links *D. setosum* density with algal cover/biomass, supporting grazing consequences but not a universal reef threshold or instant barren formation. [WA Museum, identification and behavior](https://museum.wa.gov.au/online-collections/names/Diadema-setosum); [Ishikawa et al., 2016 cage experiment](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/pbr/11/4/11_B110401/_pdf/-char/en).

**Player choice → actual consequence.** In a normal night dive, choose to follow the existing cropped edge or inspect remaining tall cover. Pin one comparison spot, resume, and follow the same resident's autonomous contact-grazing. A small boundary advances where food is actually consumed; an adjacent untouched sample remains green. Compare the two locations after this real change. Unlike the snail card, this is interpreting a local grazing mosaic, with no player sharing-meal requirement, whole-garden clearing goal or regrowth countdown.

**Code / art / bounded state.** Reuse finite coverage/contact state, with a different crop footprint and an autonomous target on the existing reachable substrate. At most two 12-cell surfaces, one resident target and two saved sample snapshots. Add a verified underside feeding pose and coherent spine/body clearance. Coverage and resource amount decrement together; clipped art must describe the actual changed cells. Crop size, routine timing and target arbitration remain stated game abstractions, not measurements from the cited experiment.

**Acceptance.** Normal night selection/travel exposes an existing s62; day entry does not spawn one for the card. Clicking a sample records an observation position and cannot command movement, raise spines or graze remotely. Actual underside contact advances the crop boundary. Save/reload and reef return retain both samples without awarding unseen progress. A busy/digesting resident permits another current observation rather than an appetite reset. No added urchins, removed predators, universal “more bare is better” meter or transferred covering behavior. The documented *Stegopontonia* association does not authorize adding unrelated s45 between these spines.

**Rank: 3 — P1 / M after shared substrate work; L without it, identity-gated.** A smaller companion observation to s61, with a distinct question and consequence. Defer shelter-return/daylight schedules until an actual within-dive time system exists.

## s46 — A home kept clear, only if it becomes a documented coral crab

**Source → hard gate.** Stewart et al.'s experiments support sediment removal by coral-associated trapeziid crabs. The Osenberg group's research explicitly studies *Trapezia serenei* and coral sediment clearing. Smithsonian describes *Trapezia* living within corals and feeding on coral tissue/mucus, incompatible with silently treating current sand-route, detritus-only s46 as that animal. Accept a specific crab–host pair only after checking the study's taxon/host details, diagnostic art, locality, diet and host occupancy. [Stewart et al., primary experiment](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00338-006-0132-7); [Osenberg Lab, named study system](https://osenberglab.ecology.uga.edu/ocean-bridges/); [Smithsonian, Trapezia](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/trapezia-crabs-watercolors).

**Player choice → actual consequence.** Inspect the identified crab's lightly dusted host. Choose a view of a branch pocket or where loosened grains land; swim there and watch autonomous claw/leg clearing. A sediment packet leaves a real branch cell and lands below. Move to the other view to connect source and destination. The crab remains autonomous; selecting a pocket changes the observer's position, not its commands. A revealed surface remains revealed, without instant coral healing or manufactured storms.

**Code / art / bounded state.** Add a host-local route and residentCare independent of meals. Share conserved sediment cells with the coral-housekeeping research while retaining crab handling and occlusion. One verified host, six source cells, six receiving cells and one active packet suffice. Save host/actor identity, cell amounts, transfer phase and the observed source/destination relationship. Art requires the selected crab skin, supported host posture and real clearing contacts; ordinary bottom-route placement cannot impersonate coral occupancy.

**Acceptance.** Real inspection/view controls reveal a conserved branch-to-floor transfer, including mid-transfer save/reload. Neither view selection nor a renderer timer completes clearing. Offscreen transfer cannot provide witness credit; a replaced host or crab requires a fresh observation. The routine remains observable without a hunger gate. No extra crab, borrowed crown-of-thorns combat, decorative-sponges collection, anemone boxing or shell-exchange claim.

**Rank: 4 — P2 / L, strict identity/host/content gate.** Strong environmental story if the product deliberately adopts a named representative; otherwise retain scavenging and defer this card.

**Alternative without a coral identity transfer:** a future verified shore-crab case study could expose leading/trailing leg contacts during an autonomous passage. Martinez et al. measured a distinctive underwater gait in *Grapsus tenuicrustatus*, including alternating substrate thrust and glide; that is species/environment evidence, not a universal sideways-crab animation. Choosing the foot view rather than a route command could reveal real planted contact and body displacement. It needs a named crab, matching rock-shore habitat and contact rig, so ranks below the four cards above. Do not transplant that gait into generic s46 or introduce a land/tide scene implicitly. [Martinez et al., primary locomotion study](https://polypedal.berkeley.edu/publications/051_Martinez_UnderwaterPuntingbyanIntertidalCrab_JExpBiol_1998.pdf).

## Shared delivery conditions

- Extend observation kinds, not the playable roster. Retain the original four journeys and discoveries; old saves default new optional records to absent and retain animal identities, physiology and resource amounts.
- Separate simulation contact/state from explanatory overlays. A choice must change the view, usable substrate or resulting route; merely selecting two labels and waiting earns nothing.
- Admit an existing reachable resident and usable site. No added adult, teleport, diet substitution, appetite refill, mandatory digestion wait or offscreen catch-up. An unavailable encounter has an explicit alternative or exit.
- Save bounded world changes and identity references. Pause/modal/blur freeze motion and progress; inactive reefs give no observation credit. Missing/replaced actors or surfaces cannot inherit evidence or duplicate committed consumption.
- Test actual Esc/inspect/Resume, pointer movement, contextual keyboard equivalents, cancellation, muted/reduced-motion presentation, save/import and reef return. Close restores ordinary player steering immediately. Any peaceful local observation protection or held-pose need suspension is explicit implementation work, not an assumption about Practice mode.

All four IDs are covered above. The order is **s61 spatial grazing → s47 contact observation → s62 cropped-edge comparison → s46 conditional host care**. These are research priorities, not promises of shipped features or claims of playtesting. Sources were checked through accessible primary/authoritative pages on 10 October 2026; WoRMS and the tube-foot articles supplied indexed records where direct access was limited.
