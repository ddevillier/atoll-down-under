## Evidence and taxonomic gates

Sources were browsed/opened on 10 October 2026. Museum forum observations are labeled as such; historical names below follow their sources and require a current taxonomic check before any roster rename.

| Evidence | Supported behavior and scope | Consequence for s60 |
|---|---|---|
| [WA Department of Primary Industries and Regional Development: Nudibranch](https://marinewaters.fish.wa.gov.au/resource/fun-fact-sheet-nudibranch/) (institutional fact sheet; page reviewed 2025) | Distinguishes head chemical sensors from external gills; describes carnivorous dietary diversity and egg masses. | Safe group-level teaching: sensory tentacles are not eyes or gills. Do not turn its broad overview into an exact species, ribbon geometry, egg count, or defensive mechanism. |
| [Rudman, 2002: head-waggling observation](https://www.seaslugforum.net/find/8367) (Australian Museum expert response to a Malaysian *Chromodoris tinctoria* observation) | Describes a raised, side-to-side anterior search and chemical sensing by rhinophores; explains similar searching in many dorids. | Strong inspiration for a restrained search pose. It is not evidence that the animal follows a visible light, mouse cursor, musical cue, or a mathematically exact plume. Use the source's historical binomial only as an example, not the roster identity. |
| [McCullagh, Bishop & Wyeth 2014, Journal of Experimental Biology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25324338/) (opened primary-study abstract) | In *Tritonia diomedea*, one rhinophore provided sufficient input for navigation consistent with odor-gated orientation to flow. | A useful limit on the older expert explanation: do not teach that all nudibranchs must compare left and right scent intensity. This different taxon does not establish the painted sponge browser's exact navigation rule. |
| [Belmonte et al. 2026, BMC Zoology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40850-026-00262-6) (opened primary full text, published 18 April 2026) | Brazilian *Cadlina rumia* in laboratory Y-maze trials responded to waterborne cues from its prey *Dysidea etheria*, conspecifics and potential predator *Pachygrapsus transversus*. Relative attraction among non-predator treatments overlapped statistically; the paper notes repeated-individual limitations. | Stronger evidence for a named sensory case study, conditional on matching slug/sponge art and an explicit Atlantic setting. Do not borrow its predator cue for the generic reef crab, guarantee each individual's choice, or turn uncertain cue preferences into a ranked flavor puzzle. |
| [Rudman & Bergquist 2007, Molluscan Research](https://mapress.com/mrs/article/view/mr.27.2.2) (opened primary review abstract, full article access restricted) | Synthesizes feeding records for 137 chromodorid species and finds specificity at species and genus levels. | “Sponge” is a gameplay category, not a claim that every sponge suits every nudibranch. No exact slug–sponge pair is established for s60. A specific prey-recognition puzzle needs species and prey identification first. |
| [Carbone et al. 2013, PLOS ONE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3631210/) (opened primary study) | Six sampled chromodorid species included *Hypselodoris infucata* and *Ceratosoma gracillimum*. Chemical analyses and feeding assays support concentrated deterrent metabolites in particular mantle structures. | Compatible direction for a verified sponge-eating chromodorid; not a stinging-cell mechanism. The study does not justify projecting a toxic cloud, requiring predator attacks, or an instant defense refill after each sponge bite. |
| [Cobb and Rudman 2009: *Chromodoris annae* eggs](https://www.seaslugforum.net/find/23001) (Australian Museum photographic observation/expert interpretation) | Queensland-collected *C. annae* deposited eggs after collection; Rudman identifies the characteristic flat ribbon. | Concrete named candidate for a future flat-ribbon observation. This is not proof of wild deposition duration, parental guarding, or the identity of the painted resident. |
| [Australian Museum: *Glaucus atlanticus*](https://australian.museum/blog/science/sea-slug-saga-2016-sleek-geeks-science-winner/) (institutional account) | Floating surface nudibranch associated with bluebottle feeding and borrowed defense. | A diversity comparison only. It cannot donate stingers, surface floating, or bluebottle feeding to this bottom-crawling sponge browser. |
| [Rudman: Solar-powered sea slugs](https://www.seaslugforum.net/find/solarpow) (Australian Museum expert fact sheet) | Separates sacoglossan retention of algal plastids from some nudibranchs retaining whole symbiotic algae; uses *Placida cf. dendritica* and *Pteraeolidia ianthina* as examples. | Do not make s60 eat algae or recharge chloroplasts. Conversely, do not teach that no nudibranch ever has photosynthetic symbionts. Neither mechanism belongs to s60 without a new verified identity and diet. |

## Strong card A — The invisible trail

**Fresh decision:** choose where to look and which direction the resident's sensory search suggests. This is observation of information, not eating, controlling the slug, or managing hunger. Its mystery is that two tiny head organs investigate a world the player cannot ordinarily see.

**Four beats:**

1. Pause with Escape, click the existing s60, and select a new “Watch its senses” observation. A short close-up shows the two front rhinophores separately from the rear gills. Select Resume watching.
2. Move the mouse as an observation lens over the nearby water. A faint, clearly labeled interpretation of chemical cues appears only inside the lens, anchored to an existing sponge patch. The animal performs an occasional small anterior sweep while remaining attached to its route.
3. Click one of two roomy “watch this side” markers to place an observation pin, based on the sweep and cue direction. This records a prediction; it does not steer the animal, move food, or count as a feeding click. A mistaken prediction is simply revisable.
4. Watch the same resident's autonomous routine turn and advance a short distance toward that existing patch. Compare its head orientation with the pin; the lens fades into an ordinary view and the journal records the sensory observation. The new search routine must actually choose its own heading; observation pins never drive it. Completion requires a witnessed search/turn, never a meal, long wait for hunger, or a correct-answer streak.

**Visible world effect:** rhinophores and head sweep, a grounded turn, and continued foot movement connect the information to a living animal. The cue particles are an explanatory overlay, explicitly not bioluminescence or visible chemicals. The resident continues its normal routine afterward. No sponge depletion, toxin refill, summoned neighbor, or score penalty.

**Hooks and bounded work:** retain `(reef, host, incarnation)` from resident observations. Add a separate observation kind/state rather than pretending `meal=true`; adapt `residentInstruction`, `beginResidentObservation`, and `tickResidentObservation`. Proposed local record: version, host identity, source patch ID, phase 0–3, prediction left/right/null, sweep witnessed, turn witnessed, completed. One active source and one pin; no recorded pointer trail. Reuse ground routes and `walkOnGround`, extending the resident pose with a small anterior sweep. Split front rhinophore motion from rear gill motion in the rig. At most a handful of renderer-only cue flecks, discarded on exit. This is **M / P1**, principally because observation controls and anatomy need honest implementation.

**Identity and evidence boundary:** generic chemical sensing is supported; the particular cue field, occasional search schedule, and two-marker prediction are authored teaching devices. Label this interpretation at entry and in its reflection. It cannot claim exact prey recognition, a universal two-rhinophore compass or experimentally measured plume-following performance. A stronger version can explicitly offer a *Cadlina rumia* / *Dysidea etheria* Atlantic case study after identity, prey, art and route gates, preserving the generic catalog and ordinary diet outside it. It should compare a prey cue with neutral water, without a predator or conspecific spawn, a forced bite or reconstructed laboratory starvation. If the source is unreachable on the existing ground route, explain that this encounter is unavailable and retain ordinary observation; do not offer indefinite searching, teleportation or a fabricated meal.

**Save, pause, offscreen:** freeze phase and pose time during pause, modal, blur, and inactive reef. Save identifiers and bounded flags; reconstruct the lens without saving particles. Older saves default the new state to absent and retain their old browsing badge. Returning preserves the same host association; if the host or patch is gone or reincarnated, explicitly offer a fresh observation without inheriting its predecessor's progress. No unseen completion. This story adds no health/energy timer; any isolated observer-mode suspension of the player's ordinary metabolism must be implemented explicitly, not assumed to exist.

**Actual-controls acceptance:** enter Practice as an eligible playable fish, find an existing s60, Escape/click it, activate observation, and resume. Verify moving the mouse now moves only the labeled lens while that explicit observation mode is active; outside it, normal swimming remains unchanged. Click each pin without `sim.eatAt` firing. Observe physical search and turn with zero meals; incorrect prediction remains peaceful and revisable. Escape pauses both motion and progress. Save/reload, change reef/return, and replace a missing host; confirm identities, no offscreen credit, and one durable discovery across replay. Close observation restores steering immediately. Test large hit targets and reduced-motion cues.

**Causal acceptance:** replay the same seeded encounter with different pins and confirm the resident's physical turn is unchanged. Its response must depend on an authored environmental cue, not the player's prediction. For a named case study, neutral and prey-cue episodes must produce readable independent behavior, with hesitation/no choice a valid observation rather than failure. Save the case-study identity and cue/site reference, not an implied change to every generic resident. A reduced-motion view retains head orientation, source location and actual displacement.

## Conditional card B — A ribbon on the rock

**Gate and priority:** **M–L / P2**, only after an explicit named-species choice with matching art, range and egg evidence. *Chromodoris annae* is an evidence-backed candidate, not a proposed silent rename. The present purple painting is not established as that species. Retaining generic s60 permits an explicitly illustrative field-guide diagram, but not a falsely exact in-world egg type.

**Fresh decision:** choose a viewing position that reveals whether a ribbon lies flat against the rock, then follow the deposition edge. No feeding task, mating commands, guard duty, or egg-cleaning claim.

**Four beats:** inspect a real eligible resident already entering an authored laying episode; choose one of two observation positions without moving the animal; move the lens along the gradually deposited ribbon to reveal its attachment and outer edge; watch the resident crawl onward, leaving the attached ribbon visible on that rock. A short authored episode may show only the final section; explicitly avoid presenting seconds as the biological duration of the full process. There is no hatching deadline, egg-loss threat, parent guarding, or spawned juvenile reward.

**World effect and hooks:** one persistent substrate-anchored ribbon, physically growing from the deposition point, plus a slow curved crawl. Integrate a laying pose/route into resident lifecycle and the observer input mode from card A. New ground-site geometry must stay reachable; do not pull the animal across the screen to a stage. Art needs a species-matched portrait, the correct flat ribbon shape, attachment detail and a deposition animation; it is more than a badge or static tooltip.

**Bounded state and lifecycle:** one site and one ribbon per active observation, progress clamped 0–1, fixed host identity, witnessed-edge flag, complete flag. Save its site, form and progress, not thousands of eggs. Pause/offscreen freeze deposition and prevent inferred credit; reload resumes the attached form. If the adult later leaves, the deposited ribbon remains a site object independent of its identity. Missing host before the witnessed finish suspends the observation; no replacement parent or instant completion. Replays do not accumulate egg objects. Normal life-history claims beyond this observed shape require further sources.

**Acceptance:** start entirely through inspect/Resume controls; demonstrate lens movement without swim/eat input leakage, deposition at the real resident's position, ribbon continuity on the same rock, and continued crawl after laying. Pause halfway, reload, leave/return, and close observation: no moving ribbon, time jump, duplicate eggs, substituted host, or lost normal controls. Complete without any sponge meal. The journal must name the selected taxon and distinguish the authored timescale.

## Defer or reject

- A stolen-stinger upgrade for the existing sponge eater: incompatible evidence and diet.
- A solar charging garden, leaf-slug chloroplast mechanic, or regeneration trick: taxonomically misplaced.
- Making bright color a toxin meter or exact species identification: unsupported.
- Farming sponges, nurturing eggs, cleaning the egg ribbon, or guarding offspring: not established by the examined sources.
- Scaring the resident repeatedly to win its trust: repeats an existing authored withdrawal, adds pressure, and teaches an unsupported relationship.
- A second sponge meal plus travel quest: already implemented; retain as the modest existing discovery if observation infrastructure is not funded.
