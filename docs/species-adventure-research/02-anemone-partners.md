## Evidence ledger (claims, scope, and limits)

1. **Nocturnal host oxygenation — direct primary experiment.** Szczebak et al. (2013), *Anemonefish oxygenate their anemone hosts at night*, JEB, DOI 10.1242/jeb.075648. Experiments used **A. bicinctus and E. quadricolor**. Paired animals in contact had higher net dark oxygen uptake than separated or mesh-separated partners; fish performed more flow-modulating behavior with hosts present. Host oxygen uptake increased over the lower tested flow range and then plateaued. Authors infer host oxygenation; combined-pair measurements do not directly isolate every partner's metabolic change. Do not advertise a universal percentage boost or reward indefinite faster fanning. [Primary abstract, opened](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23447664/); [Auburn institutional record](https://aurora.auburn.edu/handle/11200/50543?show=full); [institutional PDF](https://aurora.auburn.edu/bitstream/handle/11200/50543/970.full.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1). PDF direct opening failed; institutional indexed text and PubMed were available.

2. **Mucus is chemically protective, not a thick-skin shield.** The Australian Museum explains that hosts retain functional nematocysts and can feed while fish are among the tentacles; it discusses acquired host mucus and reduced sting-triggering components as explanations whose relevance may vary. This supports a mystery/observation beat, not a solved universal ritual. [Australian Museum](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/fishes/why-dont-anemonefishes-get-stung/).

3. **Newer mucus evidence should update future interpretation.** Roux et al. (2025), BMC Biology, found lower mucus sialic acids in sampled anemonefishes than a non-symbiotic damselfish, and developmental changes associated with protection. Laboratory mucus comparisons included **A. biaculeatus, A. clarkii, A. frenatus, A. ocellaris and A. percula**, including fish maintained without hosts. This strengthens chemical-composition explanations beyond the current museum summary. It does not establish that all hosts accept all fish, or that contact is always necessary/sufficient. [Open primary paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12915-025-02144-8).

4. **Host choice is conditional.** Nguyen et al. (2019), J Fish Biology, studied captive naive **A. ocellaris** at different juvenile stages. Newly settling and post-settlement juveniles differed in preference; later juveniles associated rapidly with their common natural host and grew better with natural-host contact. Captive outcomes cannot simply become a wild compatibility table or an adult host-switching quest. [Open primary paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jfb.13910).

5. **Acclimation does not guarantee every pairing.** Nguyen et al. (2024), J Fish Biology, studied juvenile **A. ocellaris** with six anemones. In this experiment they formed associations with natural host **Stichodactyla gigantea** and, under conditions of host removal, some alternative hosts; no association formed with **E. quadricolor, H. malu or M. doreensis**. Behaviors and timing differed between hosts. This is experimental population/context evidence, not a universal prohibition for every aquarium or population. [Open primary paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jfb.15772). Strong reason to reject a universal acclimation progress bar, and especially to avoid combining A. ocellaris host-choice results with the A. bicinctus oxygenation pair as if they were one fish.

6. **Egg tending changes the egg environment.** Green & McCormick (2005), *O2 replenishment to fish nests*, Behavioral Ecology, DOI 10.1093/beheco/ari007, reports nest oxygen rising shortly after tending, greater male care, and adjustments with development and ambient oxygen. This work is on **A. melanopus**; use species-specific context, not a fixed fanning duty cycle for the genus. [JCU primary repository abstract](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/6003/); [publisher](https://academic.oup.com/beheco/article-abstract/16/2/389/297859). Pacaro et al. (2023) separately documents nocturnal care in laboratory **A. percula**: [primary paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/eth.13384). These are substrate nests, not the cardinalfish mouthbrood already implemented.

7. **Institutional corroboration for care, with narrow use.** Georgia Aquarium's **A. percula** profile describes nest preparation and male fanning. The Australian Museum's **A. akindynos** page describes nest preparation, spawning and care by both parents with a larger male role. Use these for the broad care story, not to merge precise incubation times or entire host lists across taxa. [Georgia Aquarium](https://www.georgiaaquarium.org/animal/clown-anemonefish/); [Australian Museum](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/fishes/barrier-reef-anemonefish-amphiprion-akindynos-allen-1972/). Do not copy the aquarium's simplified causal claims about hierarchy or immunity as settled mechanisms.

8. **Social organization is real but not a promotion game.** Buston & Cant (2006) quantified **A. percula with Heteractis magnifica** in Madang Lagoon: a breeding pair with smaller nonbreeders and a structured size hierarchy. This supports observation of established neighbors. It does not support equal cooperative housemates, a universal exact ratio, or player actions to remove a dominant fish. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16794835/). No eviction, induced sex change, starvation, or death is proposed as an objective.

9. **Active host provisioning — new, direct primary evidence.** Kobayashi et al. (2025), *Active provisioning of food to host sea anemones by anemonefish*, studied **A. clarkii with E. quadricolor**. Field presentations showed size/type-dependent consumption versus delivery; satiety increased delivery. Food tracking supported likely host consumption, although not every tracked item reached the mouth. Natural delivery was seen only twice across 100 dives; do not describe it as a universal daily chore. Controlled provisioning increased host growth, which does not justify instant expansion or growth on delivery. [Open primary paper, full indexed methods/results read](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-85767-9). The freely offered large morsel in Card E is an authored encounter, not a claim of common natural abundance.

10. **The host's expansion is part of the partnership, with contextual limits.** Porat & Chadwick-Furman (2004) found different expansion and re-expansion after fish removal/return for **E. quadricolor + A. bicinctus** at Eilat. Predation and longer-term survival also mattered. This supports studying host posture, not a universal instant happiness reaction or deliberately removing partners. [Publisher primary abstract, full paper subscription-limited](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10750-004-2688-y). Use it as a scoped reflection alongside B/A; the generic game's large-passer retraction threshold remains authored. A defense adventure would require a verified anemone predator absent from this evidence-matched roster, and is deferred.

## Card A — The Night Garden (s7 care; s63 beneficiary)

**Priority P1, effort M, identity-dependent.** Hook: a quiet host appears still, but small fin movements are changing the water around its tentacles. Based on evidence 1; an explicitly identified case study is a prerequisite, not a claim about all s7/s63.

**Player loop (4 steps):** (1) Swim to the already-selected living host at night and inspect local tracer movement; choose one of two physical resting positions. (2) Settle among verified tentacle slots and watch automatic nocturnal fin/body movement begin. (3) Reposition to the other slot or swim outside the host, comparing actual water movement with and without those local movements. (4) Return to a chosen resting slot and inspect the resulting flow. This is a position-and-observe decision, not a manual fanning rhythm challenge; sleep-associated motion comes from the animal routine.

**Real control and ecological effect:** Actual position, facing and fin/body movement alter one bounded local flow model. Harmless tracer paths through the tentacles visibly differ between resting locations and open water. Tracers represent water, not spawned food; fish move water and do not manufacture oxygen. Explain the exchange implication in the reflection, or label any optional model readout explicitly as an educational model. Tentacle sway is authored feedback, not an oxygen assay. No reward for faster input.

**Reuse:** home-journey identity/route; refuge geometry; articulated fish fins; resident tentacle/retract rendering; dispatcher and journal. Avoid taking over resident meal state.

**Bounded additions:** one host reference, stage enum, one or two patch indices, local flow/response values in [0,1], two witnessed-condition flags, capped active seconds, completion flag. Add local fin flutter/short repositioning and tentacle sway; soft water swish optional, never alleged biological vocalization. No full fluid simulation.

**Acceptance:** Off-target, wrong-facing, remote clicking, idle proximity and expired host references produce no care credit. A gentle current can make no extra care necessary; observing that is a valid outcome after a real comparison. Player sees response begin and settle while present. Pause/load does not finish the comparison. Exit leaves a healthy ordinary resident. Proposed discovery/badge caption: **A Shared Breath** (one discovery; no repetitions required).

**Risk:** M/L. Largest risks are taxon overreach and missing time support. `Simulation.start` sets s7 to day; no live dusk transition currently appears in this pathway. Require a clearly presented case-study night start or preserved-population dusk transition, never a hidden period change/respawn. Do not mistake daytime feeding classification for absence of nighttime movement. Do not award the same event separately through both species.

## Card B — Room Beside the Tentacles (s63 resident observation)

**Priority P1, effort S/M; safe earlier scope.** Hook: watching well means leaving enough space to see what the resident does. Uses the game's existing retraction behavior; the response thresholds are explicitly authored, not taxonomic measurements.

**Player loop (4 steps):** (1) Inspect an existing s63 and choose either a near or farther viewing position. (2) Notice its actual response to current passing traffic; move back if it retracts. (3) Select a side that leaves its real anemonefish neighbor room to approach and settle, if a neighbor is present. (4) Observe the same host with expanded tentacles and the same fish occupying its normal refuge, and record the relationship.

**Real control/effect:** Player distance changes disturbance for eligible larger observers using current behavior. A nearby occupied corridor can briefly delay a willing fish's final approach; moving aside releases that constraint and the fish swims normally into the refuge. It is an authored spatial accommodation, not evidence that fish understand courtesy. If no partner is present, offer the ordinary resident observation and explain that a partnership has not been seen; do not summon one.

**Reuse/state/feedback:** resident focus and ID/incarnation checks, `anemoneHome`/`approachRefuge`, current tentacle retraction and fish activity labels. Add at most one fish reference, view-side enum, bounded relaxed/settled observation timers, and paired-witness flag. Use existing swim and tentacle movement, with optional quiet confirmation tone.

**Acceptance:** Only observed real occupancy and host relaxation count; selecting an empty coordinate does not. Player cannot drag the host or order a fish across reefs. Ignore/recover cleanly from meal handling, natural traffic and lost partner identity. Freeze observation on pause/offscreen. Save view choice and participants; revalidate conditions on load. Proposed one-time discovery: **Room for a Neighbor**.

**Risk:** L/M. Do not present s7's current blanket retraction exemption as universal safe host compatibility. Small s7 observers may not trigger retraction; their choice is corridor positioning, not an invisible disturbance stat. This adds spatial accommodation and two-animal observation rather than repeating resident feeding.

## Card C — A Careful Introduction (s7; host-choice mystery)

**Priority P2, effort M, defer compatibility simulation until taxa are explicit.** Hook: an anemone that looks inviting need not be the right partner. Evidence 2–5 supports uncertainty, staged approaches, and host-dependent outcomes.

**Player loop (4 steps):** (1) Inspect an existing candidate and compare its documented identity with the fish's case-study identity. (2) Choose to observe from the edge or make a cautious physical approach. (3) Respond to visible hesitation/retraction by backing out and waiting, or settle when the explicitly supported pairing permits it. (4) Record what was observed and what remains unknown; choosing not to enter is a complete valid conclusion for an unresolved pairing.

**Control/contact:** E selects an approach intent; ordinary movement and a real tentacle-edge contact gate produce the tentative-touch animation. No remote instant bonding, stamina damage or repeated-sting requirement. For unresolved generic actors, contact never grants a biologically worded immunity achievement. Keep the existing home choice available as a labeled game abstraction.

**Reuse/state/feedback:** home sites, actual host identity, movement/withdrawal, story actions. One candidate reference, stage enum, two observation flags, capped contact/calm timer and explicit evidence-scope enum; no mucus chemistry percentage. Small hover/back-away/settle poses; no new animal sound needed. No forced successful acclimation on timeout.

**Acceptance:** One actual supported introduction or a correctly scoped decision to remain outside can earn **Careful Visitor**. Record observation only once. Pause/offscreen halts actions; save preserves the same host; changed host does not inherit compatibility or contact history. Failure means unanswered observation, never a harmed animal.

**Risk:** H educationally until identity is resolved. A rigid correct-host guessing puzzle and universal host-shopping loop would contradict the evidence and should be rejected. This is not a prerequisite grind for A.

## Card D — The Nest Next Door (s7 substrate egg care)

**Priority P2/P3, effort L.** Hook: a patch beside the anemone holds an anchored clutch; care is directed at the eggs as well as at the adult host. Evidence 6–7; specify a supported species and parental role before implementation.

**Player loop (4 steps):** (1) Inspect an already-existing persistent nest at the host's rock base. (2) Compare local water movement across two parts of the clutch and choose where to fan. (3) Alternate fanning with a short real foraging break or partner handoff when that existing parent is available. (4) Return to inspect the eggs and record a completed care episode; hatching is optional, not the reward trigger.

**Control/effect:** Near-nest facing and deliberate held fanning increase a bounded local exchange indicator and move visible water across attached eggs. Treat embryos as a clutch representation; no new adults/juveniles appear to certify success. Handoff requires the existing parent actually swimming into position and beginning care. No idle click-to-hatch button or mouthbrooding pose.

**Reuse/state/feedback:** nursery module's bounded timing, close-range action and cancellation patterns only; do not reuse its spawned-young reward or mouthbrood biology. Reuse story/NPC hooks and journal. Need a persistent nest lifecycle first: nest ID, host and parent IDs/incarnations, substrate anchor, clutch presence/stage, two capped exchange values, care observations, completed flag. Eggs exist from a justified world event independent of starting this quest, or the story is unavailable. Add tiny attached-egg artwork/pectoral fin animation and optional water swish; no egg sound.

**Acceptance:** No nest means no story start and no fabricated clutch. Move out of range and care stops. New IDs cannot inherit a nest; save/reload preserves attachment and parent identity. Offscreen/paused time produces neither progress nor loss. One observed care episode earns **Nest Tender**, with no clutch-count grind. Existing parent can resume ordinary care when player leaves.

**Risk:** M biology / H scope. Current s7 has no verified species, sex or breeding lifecycle. Do not quietly designate the player male. Prefer an explicit supported-role story or observation of an established parent. Do not turn a week of development into an unexplained minute, make young die on abandonment, or duplicate Dad's Big Mouth.

## Card E — A Mouthful for the Host (s7 delivery; s63 tentacle-to-mouth response)

**Priority P0 after explicit identity gates; effort M/L.** Biological basis: evidence 9. This adds physical transfer to a living host, rather than another plankton trip, passive food-sharing click, or resident meal counter.

**Choice and loop:** (1) Inspect the named host and a nearby loose animal morsel; compare a swallowable small bite with a larger piece suited to carrying. (2) Choose to eat an ordinary eligible small bite or take the offered larger piece for the host. (3) Approach one of two reachable oral-disc edges and release at tentacle contact; edge choice changes the host's visible transfer route. (4) Give the tentacles room and inspect the same food moving toward the same host's mouth. One successful observed delivery is sufficient; no repeat quota or forced feeding to satiety. The size comparison and two release routes are game interpretation.

**Actual consequence:** Pickup moves one finite resource from world to mouth ownership; release transfers it to the host's capture surface. Tentacles then carry that same item inward, followed by an actual host commit. Player calories accrue only for the normal swallowed bite. No instant host growth, healing, universal thank-you pose, live prey harm, spawned partner, or invented mucus reward. A natural-feeding interpretation needs an already available loose animal resource; an intentionally offered morsel must be labeled as a research-inspired encounter, not concealed behind an ordinary wild-food claim.

**Code/art/state:** Reuse `feeding-contact.js` landmarks, persistent host identity, resident meal commit/handling, and `gramma-nest.js`'s mouth-attached carry pattern; lining remains nonfood and is not reused as nutrition. Need resource ID/amount ownership (`world`, `carried`, `captured`, `consumed`), host reference, two authored capture sockets, bounded tentacle-transfer progress, witnessed-contact/commit flags, phase and one discovery. Author verified fish/host paintings, separable tentacles and a painted morsel. Existing s63 center-mouth reach and `.22`-second commit cannot by themselves depict transport from a tentacle edge; add real constrained transfer geometry. Compressed handling is an explicit game timing choice, never a biological swallowing-time claim.

**Acceptance:** Far clicks select/travel without pickup or credit. Morsel follows the displayed mouth while carrying, then stays at the real tentacle socket and follows the host's inward path. Wrong-host/occupied handling causes an honest retry. Both placement edges produce different visible routes with the same bounded resource. Cancel/drop leaves the item at its physical location; pause/offscreen freezes transfer. Save/load at all four ownership stages never duplicates food, commits twice, loses identity or awards twice. No host means unavailable; a replaced ID cannot inherit the captured item. The resident-observer version may witness an eligible existing named fish deliver, but never promotes s63 to playable or adds a second badge for the same event.

**Prerequisites/verdict:** Strongest new s7 mechanic once the exact pair, transferable animal-food resource and animated tentacle capture are authored. Existing diets are abstract compatibility labels, not evidence for carrying arbitrary algae/sponges. Avoid mandatory appetite manipulation and a universal delivery probability. Keep the generic current home journey intact.

## Deferred / rejected directions

- Nutrient exchange is a useful reflection alongside A, but passive waste production is a weak new player decision and invisible fertilization percentages would mislead. Keep as scoped explanatory context unless a real resource/host model is justified.
- Host cleaning can be mentioned using carefully scoped evidence, but a generic remove-parasites minigame would duplicate the Reef Clinic and overstate what all s7 do.
- Social ranking is best an optional observation of persistent neighbors; no promotion, eviction or forced reproductive succession objectives. Current same-species catalog sizes cannot prove a biological hierarchy.
- Do not turn anemones into universal oxygen generators, freely relocating flowerpots, or free-host vending machines. No invented danger solely to make care feel useful.
- Immediate low-scope option is B; strongest new full adventure is now E after identity/resource gates, followed by A after identity/night gates. C and D retain their explicit prerequisites. No runtime or asset changes were made in this research pass.
