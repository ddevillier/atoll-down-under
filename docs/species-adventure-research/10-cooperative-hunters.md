# Grouper and moray adventures: evidence before cooperation

Research date: 10 October 2026. Assigned roster: s16, s26, s38, s39. Design only; no runtime, asset, audio, or save changes made.

## Recommendation

Prioritize **movement-linked appearance** (s39, M/P1), **a reversible crevice passage** (s26, M–L/P1), and **bottom rest versus a hovering lookout** (s38, M/P1). For s16, a **territorial visit** supplies a more distinct social possibility than another ambush; keep it conditional on the exact behavioral and encounter-admission gates below (M/P2). The ledge-approach idea remains an improvement to the existing hunt, rather than the recommended standalone adventure. Small evidence/copy corrections are S/P0. Do not ship a generic grouper–moray team-up for these four species. An exact-species cooperative-hunt expansion for s39 is possible research-wise, but its documented moray partners are absent from this roster: L/P3, conditional on a separately scoped species expansion.

S = focused copy/state change; M = one bounded playable mechanic with modest visual support; L = new geometry, species, or multi-actor orchestration requiring a separate sprint. P0 = prerequisite correction, P1 = next-sprint candidate, P3 = deferred exploration. These are estimates, not commitments to implement all four in one sprint.

**Additional exact-taxon social evidence:** [Shpigel and Fishelson 1991, primary study, Journal of Fish Biology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1095-8649.1991.tb03628.x) includes *C. miniata* in compound territories with male/female social groups and female subterritories. Its abstract reports male patrols, visits and antiparallel swimming across the study. The accessible abstract does not assign every movement observation to each of the three studied species; inspect the full species-separated ethogram before claiming that a particular *C. miniata* visit gesture is reconstructed. This supports investigating a social chapter, not forced pair bonding, a full harem-management chore, or universal territorial dimensions.

The classic [Bshary et al. 2006 primary paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.0040431) principally studies *Plectropomus pessuliferus* and *Gymnothorax javanicus*. Its success rates, head-shake recruitment, and complementary tactics are not evidence for the s16–s26 pair. Likewise, findings on *Plectropomus leopardus* must not be attached to s39 merely because its display name says “Coral trout.” Absence of suitable evidence here is not a claim that a behavior is impossible.

**Partnership recheck:** the 2006 paper also mentions two *V. louti* signaling observations, including one following artificial food presentation; these do not establish a general partnership or the 2025 study's natural joined hunts. The newer study records head stripes directed toward *C. miniata*, but explicitly leaves recipients' perception and response unresolved: a stripe is not a confirmed “call” or fear power. [Sampaio et al. 2024, Nature Ecology & Evolution](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-024-02525-2) includes *V. louti* in octopus–fish hunting groups. That is a different association involving *Octopus cyanea* and other absent taxa; it does not identify s16, s26 or s38 as interchangeable partners. Do not substitute our cuttlefish for an octopus or our yellow goatfish for a differently identified study goatfish.

Correct [publisher article](https://academic.oup.com/beheco/article/36/2/araf005/7964494), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/beheco/araf005), and [author institutional abstract](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/dynamic-color-change-in-the-grouper-variola-louti-during-interspe-3/) agree on identity and movement context. ## s16 preferred conditional card — A visit between territories

**Observed/source → choice:** the 1991 territorial study above gives *C. miniata* a social alternative to its existing meal loop. Offer an explicitly labeled male-visitor role for one short encounter: choose which of two authored approaches to use when visiting a female's subterritory, or remain at the outer viewing edge. Admission must identify a real eligible conspecific and a suitable neighboring patch first. Exact visit posture awaits the full-text gate; route spacing is our interpretation.

**Consequence:** physically approach, allow the resident to keep its patch, then perform one supported counter-directed passing movement if the verified ethogram supports it. Both animals separate to their own locations afterward. The wide approach and inner approach produce different visible spacing, rather than points for winning a chase. Completion records a visible visit and departure; no meal, spawning, eggs, automatic bond, submission, injury or claim that every resident accepts a visitor.

**Files/art/state:** optional story dispatch in `reef-story-data.js`/`reef-stories.js`, NPC reservation in `simulation.js`, local movement in `ecology.js` and authoritative body poses in the renderer. One reef, two approaches, one phase enum, one role, one neighbor `{reef,id,incarnation,species}`, bounded pass progress and completion flag. Two spotted-body poses and foreground patch cues; no new population or costume. Do not infer sex from sprite color or size. Preserve existing relationships; reserve an available adult within the initial population budget before offering this guided scene.

**Acceptance/gates:** full-text species attribution first; then show Begin only with a reachable patch and admitted participant. Real mouse/E/buttons must change approach and actual spacing. Cancel before contact restores ordinary swimming without snapping either animal. Save each phase, replace the neighbor and revisit the reef: no recycled identity, automatic partner replacement or offscreen visit credit. No eligible conspecific in an old save means an honest unavailable encounter, not endless searching. **M/P2, conditional**. Retain the existing ambush as the useful fallback.

## s16 retained enrichment — Which ledge faces the passing fish?

**New decision:** stay at a ledge whose opening faces an actual prey approach, or leave and choose a better-facing ledge. This adds reading direction and geometry to an existing timed ambush; it does not lure or summon fish.

**Four beats:** (1) Explore two clearly marked ledge mouths and observe live small-fish movement. (2) Swim near one and press E; aim toward its open side. (3) Let a fish pass outside the useful approach, or click an eligible fish when it enters that side’s short strike sector. (4) After the attempt, choose to reset here or relocate; a first correctly positioned attempt can complete the discovery even if the fish escapes.

**Observable result:** an actual prey fish continues its normal route or avoids an exposed approach. The selected ledge’s rim and strike sector explain why it was reachable; there is no guaranteed victim and no “successful cooperation” text. A s16 NPC can later reuse the same two-position wait/short-approach state without acquiring food remotely.

**Hooks and budget:** extend `ambushSites`, aim/strike eligibility and an optional story module; reuse real `suitableMeal` filtering and meal contact. One reef, two authored mouth orientations, one selected site, one phase, one bounded aim/settle timer and one attempt flag; at most one live prey reference. Art: two small ledge-mouth overlays and a subtle directional rim, preserving the grouper’s spotted identity. M/P2 as an existing-journey improvement; no new species or ability.

**Acceptance through controls:** E near either ledge changes the selected opening; pointer movement rotates aim without swimming; clicking outside the sector never teleports the fish or credits a catch; E/clicking open water releases aim. Try an empty scene: show “No passing fish nearby” and allow another ledge or normal exploration. Escape mid-settle and save/load mid-strike must not advance or replay a strike. Revisit after prey replacement: old ID alone cannot earn progress.

## s26 card — The passage through the coral

**New decision:** use a visible crevice passage to reach a nearby feeding opening, or take the longer outside route. The choice concerns the eel’s route and body, not recruiting another hunter.

**Four beats:** (1) Choose a cave with E and inspect two visible exits. (2) Move to the crevice mouth and press E to enter a short, reversible passage. (3) Steer toward either endpoint; emerge and inspect the existing nearby food situation, optionally clicking a real eligible meal. (4) Return through either route and rest at the chosen cave; discovering both entrances is sufficient, so food scarcity is not a lock.

**Observable result:** the same eel body visibly threads behind a foreground rock mask and emerges at the other opening. Existing outside fish remain outside; no fish is flushed out by an unseen scripted event. A nearby moray NPC may independently traverse an unoccupied passage, giving an observable example rather than a summonable partner. Occupied passages offer the outside route; the player cannot evict an NPC.

**Hooks and budget:** retain `homeJourney` anchors/homecoming but add a separate passage traversal state; `REEFS.shelters` alone supplies no tunnel geometry. Two entrances and a short sampled curve per selected cave, max two passages in one reef, progress clamped 0–1, one optional occupant reference. Art requires a foreground mask and continuous body/bend drawing; if existing eel art cannot bend, that becomes the first feasibility gate. The full body sweep, entrance width and occlusion must use the same authored geometry: a rigid sprite translated between mouth markers fails this card. No whole-reef collision mesh. M–L/P1 for this narrow form; L if generalized into many connected tunnels.

**Evidence limit:** crevice occupancy is documented; exact passage width, speed and route benefit are authored. Keep fish/crustacean diet claims separate from the broad game menu. Do not use a breathing gape as an aggressive command or a cooperative signal.

**Acceptance through controls:** move to entrance/E enters; steering toward the entry reverses; E exits at the nearest safe endpoint with a visible short movement, never an instant cross-reef teleport. Passage and outside path both work. Esc freezes body/progress; save/load restores the same position paused; changing reefs clears local occupancy without completing the route. NPC death/repopulation releases its reservation using ID plus incarnation. Space retains camouflage, not “call grouper.”

## s38 card — A giant’s point of view

**New decision:** watch from the bottom or hover above the ledge according to where the live food can actually be seen and reached. This uses body clearance and viewing position, instead of a longer version of the existing settle-and-eat loop.

**Four beats:** (1) Arrive beside a broad ledge and inspect bottom and hovering markers. (2) Choose one with E and settle, with the large body remaining visibly outside narrow shelter. (3) Aim at a real candidate; decide whether to approach now or move to the other viewing position when rock interrupts the view. (4) Make a short approach or let the animal pass, then choose the comfortable position for the return. Discovering an unobstructed view from both positions completes the optional story; feeding remains voluntary.

**Observable result:** the body rises into a stable hover or settles near the substrate; a single foreground rock edge blocks the sight line from one position. Existing NPCs remain visible where the geometry permits and keep normal avoidance/feeding behavior. No new aura, universal fear radius or smaller-fish submission is implied. A s38 NPC can demonstrate the same rest/hover poses with modest local movement.

**Hooks and budget:** add two altitude variants to one watch site, reuse `aimWatch`, focus identity and watched-target validation. One shallow occlusion segment, two poses, selected pose enum, two-bit observation mask, existing bounded notice timer; at most one focus reference. Art: body orientation/fin movement and ledge occluder; no growth stages or rework of giant-grouper anatomy. M/P1; pose-only visual prototype S/P2 is useful but is not the full decision.

**Acceptance through controls:** E at the chosen marker starts the correct pose; aim mode does not drag the hovering player; E releases it. A rock-obscured candidate cannot be “noticed” through the ledge, whereas changing position reveals that same real animal. Space remains passive crush and must not trigger a hovering power. The giant remains too large for normal shelter hiding. Pause/save/reef changes must preserve earned view flags but never accumulate noticing time or create a replacement target offscreen.

## s39 card — Colors on the move

**New decision:** take a slower, reef-close approach or swim above the reef and cover distance faster, then notice how the lyretail’s appearance responds. Pattern change is automatic and coupled to movement; this must not duplicate the existing cuttlefish Artist’s manual pattern/background selection.

**Observed direction:** the primary study associates bottom rest with a darker, more patterned body, and higher swimming with a brighter, more uniform body. A two-state game crossfade simplifies several independently varying pattern components. It is not evidence that the player consciously selects these patterns or that the change guarantees concealment. Keep the competitive head stripe out of a solitary movement lesson.

**Four beats:** (1) Begin beside a real reef edge, keeping the yellow-edged crescent tail visible. (2) Choose the reef-close route and settle briefly; observe the body pattern transition. (3) Choose the higher route, optionally use the ordinary dash, and observe the second appearance during movement. (4) Return along either route and inspect the two remembered states; an ordinary real meal can interrupt without invalidating exploration.

**Observable result:** the player and any present s39 NPC use the same movement-dependent visual states, so a passer can visibly change as it rises or settles. No special NPC observer must spawn to grade camouflage, and no promise of invisibility or guaranteed prey capture follows from color. The route choice itself changes travel distance and posture; avoid asserting a measured predator-detection benefit from correlational evidence.

**Hooks and budget:** optional story plus patrol-position/range helpers and renderer appearance overlay; ordinary movement supplies speed and distance from the authored substrate. Two appearance presets with bounded interpolation, one two-bit observation mask, one reef route pair and a timer; no partner reference needed. Art: carefully masked body bars/patches, preserving fin outline, spots and yellow tail margin. Reduced motion can use a short crossfade with text state labels. M/P1, subject to art legibility at game scale.

**Acceptance through controls:** mouse steering alone can produce both states; Space remains dash and is optional; E starts/ends the activity rather than manually painting the fish. Inspect with Esc during a transition: timers freeze and the label still identifies *Variola louti*. Save/load retains observations without replaying an award; offscreen NPC appearance is recomputed from its actual resumed movement, not aged through a hidden performance. Empty reef still permits the route exploration.

**Deferred cooperation card:** only after adding and verifying *G. griseus* or *G. nudivomer*, their local habitat and independently available NPC behavior, consider choose a willing partner → brief signal → inspect a shared real crevice → each pursues actual prey → separate/rest. No forced meal sharing or guaranteed response. One consenting/available partner and one prey ID+incarnation pair; leave/cleaning/death/reef change cancels the association. L/P3. This is not permission to relabel s26 or replace its art/identity.

## Shared delivery gates

1. Implement as optional Practice stories; preserve the four current journeys and earned progress. Register and validate new bounded state explicitly rather than stuffing objects into existing generic arrays. Practice retains ordinary risk systems: the story must not create attackers, timed failure, compulsory kills, or new damage as a teaching device.
2. Real contact remains necessary for any meal. Observation and route discoveries can complete without feeding. No spawned guaranteed prey, forced companionship, fake offscreen completion, or journal text claiming an event that never occurred.
3. Esc/P, modal pause, window blur and paused restoration freeze every new timer and movement. Only active-reef simulation advances local scenes. Save IDs reference actual sites; entity references always include reef, ID and incarnation, then revalidate alive/species/availability.
4. Stopping a story releases aim, targets, passage occupancy and cosmetic overrides safely. Species switches cannot carry eel passages, giant hover state, or lyretail patterns onto another fish. Older saves receive absent/default story state without resetting existing discoveries.
5. Manual browser acceptance must use the real HUD/buttons and mouse/E/Space/Esc/Save controls, not only direct simulation calls. Include interrupted aim, occupied/empty sites, one unavailable prey, save while paused, reload, leave/return and species change. Verify no completion is credited by pause or missing actors. This research-only deliverable has not run those future implementation tests.
6. A required passage, visit stance or hover needs an explicit local peaceful interaction policy: preserve hunger/health, suspend drain while the pose is mandatory, and let nearby predators physically steer around bound participants. Cancellation restores free movement before ordinary risk resumes. This is a bounded scene contract, not a silent whole-reef immunity or removal of predators.
