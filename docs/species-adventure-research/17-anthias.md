# Anthias: s11 and s58

Research date: 2026-10-10. Research/design only; no runtime change. Priority scale: P1 = good next candidate after stated gates; P2 = reserve; P3 = do not build now. Effort: S/M/L are implementation estimates, not confidence grades.

## Finding and recommendation

Both IDs already have an enacted school meal and regroup loop. Do not commission another current-following plankton trip, refuge-and-return loop, or more-neighbors counter. The genuinely different opportunity is **choosing and observing a mixed-species association**, with recognizable individuals moving into one shared formation. The exact companion identities matter: the evidence does not establish that these two playable anthias school together. Build at most one mixed-school mechanic first; the other ID should be a source-specific variation, not a second priority feature.

Recommend s58 as the first conditional reserve (M with companion persistence already available, otherwise L; P2 after companion/art and locality gates). Keep s11 as a regional variation of that mechanic (M on shared machinery; P2), and defer a standalone commission (L, P3). If the project cannot add a scientifically identified, non-playable companion, retain both current adventures and put the new evidence in the natural-history entry instead. There is no strong independent lifecycle adventure ready from the evidence inspected.

## Evidence ledger

All links checked on the research date. Source facts are separated below from proposed game rules.

1. **Taxonomic bridge, primary revision.** Anthony C. Gill (2022), [Revised definitions of the anthiadine fish genera Mirolabrichthys Herre and Nemanthias Smith](https://www.mapress.com/zt/article/view/zootaxa.5092.1.2), Zootaxa 5092:41–66, DOI 10.11646/zootaxa.5092.1.2. Rechecked publisher abstract: *tuka* remains in the newly restricted *Mirolabrichthys*; *dispar* and the proposed partner *bartlettorum* move to *Nemanthias*. This bridges old names without extending one species' behavior to another. [Anderson's primary checklist update](https://mapress.com/zt/article/download/zootaxa.5195.6.5/48821/54849), full PDF text inspected, independently confirms those combinations and retains *waitei* as the type species of *Luzonichthys*.
2. **s11 exact institutional account.** D. J. Bray (2025), Museums Victoria/OzFishNet, [Nemanthias dispar](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/4390). Opened full entry. It places feeding aggregations above upper outer-reef edges and drop-offs in moderate currents, and describes sex-associated fin and color differences. Supports current setting and possible art, not a novel feeding task, measured schooling distances, a sex-change schedule, or spawning behavior.
3. **s58 exact institutional account.** D. J. Bray (2023), Museums Victoria/OzFishNet, [Mirolabrichthys tuka](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/4403). Opened full entry. Reports outer-slope/drop-off aggregations, zooplankton feeding, female-to-male sex change and a group containing a male, females and juveniles. Its diagnostic sex-associated markings could inform future art. This establishes a lifecycle fact but supplies no exact timing, recruitment trigger, individual recognition method, or care of offspring.
4. **Exact social association, institution-hosted illustrated review.** J. E. Randall (2005), Bishop Museum author, [A Review of Mimicry in Marine Fishes](https://zoolstud.sinica.edu.tw/issue.php?id=1471), Zoological Studies 44:299–328; [paper PDF, pp. 324–325, figs. 118–123](https://zoolstud.sinica.edu.tw/Journals/44.3/299.pdf). The journal page was opened; search indexing returned the relevant institutional PDF passage and figure captions. Direct PDF rendering failed because the host serves an unsupported content type, so figure pixels were not inspected. It reports *P. dispar* in yellow/pink mixed aggregations in the Line/Phoenix Islands, and illustrates *P. tuka* with *Luzonichthys waitei* in Sulawesi. It cites Randall & McCosker (1993), **Social mimicry in fishes**, which was identified bibliographically but not opened in full. Treat the observed associations as supported; do not claim controlled proof of their benefit, an instantaneous color-changing mechanism, or Australia-wide frequency. Full figure inspection remains an art gate.
5. **s58 diet cross-check.** Academia Sinica, [Taiwan Fish Database: Pseudanthias tuka](https://fishdb.sinica.edu.tw/taxon/382602-fishdb). Opened institutional entry; reports groups on outer reef slopes and planktonic crustaceans/fish eggs as food. No justification for a selective-prey minigame or for making fish eggs into a new mandatory food resource.
6. **s11 association caution, primary experiment/field study.** [Mimicry, colour forms and spectral sensitivity of the bluestriped fangblenny, Plagiotremus rhinorhynchos](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2660993/) reports orange fangblenny associations in a pooled *P. huchtii* / *P. dispar* category and depicts measured *P. dispar* coloration. This is evidence about a different fish's mimicry, not evidence that s11 changes color on command. It is also not a peaceful mutual-benefit hook; do not repurpose this as s11 “helping a friend” gameplay.

7. **s58 recruitment, primary field survey.** [Abesamis & Russ 2010, institutional author copy](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/16931/1/Abesamis_Russ_CoralReefs_2010.pdf), *Patterns of recruitment of coral reef fishes in a monsoonal environment* ([JCU record](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/16931/)), includes *P. tuka* among abundant new recruits in the Philippines. This supports a recruitment research lead, not parental escort, a breeding trigger, an owned coral home, or a player-controlled seasonal arrival. A juvenile/recruitment story is deferred pending exact microhabitat/development evidence and a life-stage system.

Aquarium care pages appeared in search, but their feeding schedules, captive dominance advice and unsourced spawning narratives were excluded. No exact-taxon egg-guarding, parental escort, nest building, lunar spawning calendar, or settlement navigation was verified here. The exact institutional accounts and publisher abstract were reopened in this pass. Randall's indexed institutional passages and Figure 123 caption again identify the Sulawesi association; direct PDF opening still failed, so no figure pixels are claimed inspected.

## Strong conditional card — s58: A school with two kinds of neighbor

**Fresh decision.** Approach a group that looks uniformly purple/yellow, or follow its less obvious splitfin neighbor along the edge. The player chooses a viewing/joining position that reveals two species in one moving group. Completion depends on an actual association and separation being visible, not eating again or clicking two guide entries.

**Source hooks.** Ledger 4 supports the particular *tuka*–*L. waitei* association. Ledger 3 supports *tuka*'s identity and outer-slope setting. All invitation behavior, timings and formation thresholds below are game abstractions. Present the vignette as inspired by an observation from Sulawesi; do not label it a verified local Great Barrier Reef interaction without local evidence.

**Four-beat loop.**

1. In an optional outer-slope vignette, an existing purple-anthias group is approached by a correctly illustrated *L. waitei* cameo. The invitation appears only when the player is nearby and both parties are unoccupied and safe.
2. Steer to either side of the loose group. Press E or the visible context button to join its edge. One side gives a clear view of the splitfin; the other is closer to the anthias. There is no wrong side or hunger cost for investigating.
3. Maintain a relaxed position while the cameo and at least two anthias form a shared, aligned moving cluster. The cameo remains separately identifiable by silhouette and an optional name label. Its steering changes visibly from approaching to joining; it is never teleported or attached rigidly to the player.
4. Follow the splitfin toward a nearby open viewing point, or remain with the anthias as it peels away. Both choices reveal the same two identities. After the association has visibly formed and separated, the journal records “Two kinds of fish can share a school.” NPCs return to ordinary routines.

**Visible world effect.** One small mixed formation joins, changes course and separates in the scene. NPC independence is the result; food, health, protection and rewards are not fabricated. Replaying can reverse the cameo's approach side without spawning another permanent animal. Do not require feeding by either side, and do not award survival protection as if measured by the source.

**Bounded implementation and art.** One authored site per eligible section, one cameo, two to four existing anthias; one association at a time. State: version, species ID, reef/site ID, stage 0–3, cameo identity and incarnation, at most four member identities, formation/visibility dwell capped at a small design duration, joined/separated flags. Requires a verified *L. waitei* sprite with its split dorsal fin readable at actual game scale, source-checked female-pattern *tuka* reference art, a modest formation cue, and text identification that does not depend on color alone. Introduce that reference form explicitly; do not silently turn the single free-play sprite into a sex/life-stage system. No population simulator, automatic births, predator choreography or generated audio. M effort assumes cameo entity/save support already exists; otherwise L.

**Files and absent taxa.** Proposed dedicated `anthias-association.js` and renderer; explicit registration in `reef-story-data.js`, `reef-stories.js`, `simulation.js`, `main.js`, `render.js`, validated saves and the journal/discovery narration path. *L. waitei* is absent from the 64-species roster and needs its own non-playable species definition, art, bounded persistence and population rules. A recolored s11 or a mislabeled s58 is not an acceptable substitute. Preserve existing free-play assets and conspecific `validSchoolWave` behavior.

**Save, pause, offscreen and identity.** Save only validated bounded state and the extant cameo, not a duplicate spawn request. Old saves omit the optional state safely. Pause, menu and loss of visibility freeze progress; leaving the section suspends/resets the short association without penalty or unattended completion. On return, validate exact identity plus incarnation; a replacement NPC cannot satisfy the old member reference. If a member dies, feeds, seeks service, hides or flees, release the formation and allow a later reattempt. Never force a hungry animal to stay for the photograph. Use simulation time only; no offline elapsed-time progress.

**Real-controls acceptance.** Start a normal Practice dive as Purple anthias, enable the optional goal, steer with mouse movement, and invoke E/context button at the authored invitation. Confirm choosing both sides works and mouse movement can always leave. Escape/P pauses; paused creature clicks inspect, so they must not complete the active association. Space remains Burst swim. Verify at 1600×900 and 1024×768 that the splitfin silhouette and cue are visible. Save/load between beats with Ctrl+S and Save/load UI; cross the habitat edge, return, and inspect member continuity. Complete through UI actions without console-setting journey flags or teleporting participants. Assert the regular plankton adventure still works, no food/energy grant occurs, and repeated starts do not grow the entity count. These are proposed acceptance checks, not tests run in this research pass.

**Verdict.** Conditional reserve, P2, M/L. Art identity, companion persistence and the explicitly regional vignette are release gates. If those are out of scope, use a guide note only.

## s11 reserve — Many colors, one gathering

**Opportunity and scope.** A Line/Phoenix Islands vignette can show the peach fairy basslet beside the particular yellow/pink companions reported in ledger 4. It must not substitute the purple anthias as the partner or place an unsupported regional assemblage into every Australian habitat. Suggested minimum partner is *Nemanthias bartlettorum* (the paper's *Pseudanthias bartlettorum*); its current combination is verified by Gill and Anderson above. Companion range/art still need a full reference check. Do not represent color matching as a button-triggered talent: the inspected source does not establish its timescale or mechanism.

**Absent companion boundary.** *N. bartlettorum* is absent. *L. whitleyi*, *L. tapeinosoma* and *E. midas* are also absent, but unnecessary for the minimum two-species vignette. Do not commission all four or substitute existing similarly colored fish. *L. waitei* belongs to the s58 example, not automatically the s11 association.

**Fresh decision and four beats.** Notice a similar-colored newcomer; choose to stay with familiar basslets or approach the newcomer; swim beside the temporary mixed formation long enough to see different fin outlines; follow either group when the formation loosens. The NPC gathering visibly changes membership; no extra meal requirement. An optional close inspection explains that similar appearance need not mean the same species. This is the same reusable mechanic as the s58 card and should not be sold as a separate large feature.

**Implementation boundary.** Reuse the s58 bounded association contract with an exact partner allowlist and no more than one additional cameo. Needs source-verified partner art and regional *dispar* coloration. Preserve s11's existing stable player ID, ability, food and sex-unspecified sprite; a future local art variation is not a species or sex transition. Save/pause/offscreen/identity rules and real-controls acceptance are the same as above, tested separately for Peach fairy basslet at both viewports. Never use `validSchoolWave` as a loophole to make all schoolers compatible.

**Verdict.** P2 regional variation on shared infrastructure (M); P3 defer as a standalone commission (L). Strong enough for a carefully bounded regional observation; not enough for instant social color switching, a new sex-change activity, or a second feeding-wave quest. If no regional cameo setting is wanted, retain **Out for plankton** and add a short sourced natural-history comparison instead (S).

## Lifecycle and social-role guardrails

The institutional *tuka* account explicitly supports protogyny. A concise optional guide explanation can say that some individuals change from female to male during life. Do not make the player remove a male, compete for rank, “earn” a sex, rescue a supposedly incomplete group, or equate body form with courage, status or personality. No timed transformation or breeding task is ready without additional exact-taxon reproductive evidence and a deliberately designed life-stage system. The current single sprite also means a form-comparison activity is not ready merely because its prose exists.

For both fish, source-backed sexual dimorphism is useful art/reference material, not evidence that the player should command another animal's biology. The peaceful adventure decision is where to swim and what association to observe; animal welfare, sex, and social rank are not victory conditions.

s11's sourced moderate-current setting can inform ordinary station holding once current affects actual locomotion. A few directional particles above another plankton patch are insufficient for a distinct story. No exact *tuka* current optimum was verified, so do not transfer s11's preference to s58. Neither candidate needs another refuge-and-return loop. This pass edited only this research document; no implementation, art, audio or runtime testing was performed.
