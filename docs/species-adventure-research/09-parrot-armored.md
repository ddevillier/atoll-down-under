## s15 — Preserve the engineer; gate the night adventure

**Code audit.** `src/data.js` gives algae/coral, daytime activity and crushing. `src/adventures.js:33` already asks for two algae patches and a return. `src/sandcastle-engineer.js` goes much further: up to seven persistent patch records, visible sand deposition, actual algae-resource depletion and renewal, depart/return checks, and an existing compatible neighbor invited to take a real mouth-contact bite. Paused and offscreen patches retain clocks. Do not propose sand production, patch recovery, another grazer invitation, or a return-to-first-patch loop as new work.

`src/natural-history.js` explicitly leaves species unidentified and now describes simplified sand deposition and algae renewal, authored quantities/timings and no coral growth or grinding-anatomy simulation. The previously proposed journal correction is already present. Preserve it and the unresolved identity rather than repeating that task.

**Source and scope.** [Smithsonian Ocean, Tough Teeth and Parrotfish Poop](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/fish/tough-teeth-and-parrotfish-poop) supports the general link between feeding, carbonate sand, and algae removal; it does not identify the game's sprite. A field study of 225 parrotfishes across 11 species found variable night-site use and both rare/absent and facultative cocoon production. Therefore “every parrotfish makes a protective sleeping bag” is unsuitable. [Pickholtz et al. 2023, research-portal abstract](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/confronting-the-nocturnal-problem-in-coral-reefs-sleeping-site-se/) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00338-023-02389-1)).

[Grutter et al., published online 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3061186/) experimentally found reduced gnathiid attacks in cocooned fish identified by that paper as *Chlorurus sordidus*. This does not establish protection against every parasite, universal predator invisibility, or cocoon behavior for the unnamed game fish. Keep the study's taxon separate from s15 until identity and matching art are established.

**Conditional card: A bed for the night — M/Hold.** Fresh decision: leave a resting place open, or commit to cocoon construction when the eventual verified species supports it. Five beats: inspect two existing resting places; choose one and enter it; settle and form a visible envelope; choose to remain or break out; leave a small dissolving trace and record the observed behavior. The behavioral payoff is the change from swimming to enclosed rest, not a food reward or a mandatory full-night wait. No damage challenge, spawned parasites, or assertion of universal predator invisibility.

**Bounded implementation, only after identity work.** Add a separate optional story alongside Engineer through `reef-story-data.js`, `reef-stories.js`, shelter/geometry and save validation. One shelter reference, actor identity, phase enum, formation 0–1 and one capped residue; require named-species art, a translucent body-fitting cocoon and an exit-breakup pose. Player/body occupancy must precede formation. Pause/offscreen freezes progression; returning revalidates the same reef/shelter and actor identity; changing species releases enclosure. Formation and release remain interruptible. A local night scenario must preserve the current dive and cannot silently create a world day/night simulation. If protection is added later, obtain exact host/parasite evidence and apply it only while the actual envelope exists. Acceptance: unsupported species cannot start; proximity without body fit cannot complete; exiting visibly opens the envelope before free swimming; no offscreen formation or completion. Next action is identity research, not a generic parrotfish cocoon implementation.

## s22 — A little jet beneath the sand

**Code audit.** `src/natural-history.js:25` identifies A. nigropunctatus. `src/data.js` accepts snail/crab/sponge/coral. `src/foraging-journeys.js:10` currently sequences snail then sponge. Its shell phase uses `approachMeal`, requires 1.5 seconds of uninterrupted mouth contact, and consumes the real target; the second site must be at least 120 pixels away. `src/sand-search.js` only recognizes s9/s23, while existing sand sites expose worm/shrimp. Merely switching this puffer to that helper would both duplicate s23 and mismatch its present diet.

**Evidence.** [Kubota, Sato & Sakai 2026, Coral Reefs](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00338-026-02961-5), published September 25, directly observed A. nigropunctatus blowing water from the mouth to uncover sand-buried prey at Sesoko Island, Okinawa. The prey were not identified; crustaceans and gastropods are plausible from earlier diet studies, not identified outcomes of those jet observations. The paper also reports coral, algal-matrix and sponge feeding. Do not present a snail revealed by every jet as a field result. [Fishes of Australia](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/Home/species/850) independently supports a varied invertebrate/algal diet; [Australian Museum](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/fishes/blackspotted-pufferfish-arothron-nigropunctatus-bloch-schneider-1801/) establishes species identity and coral feeding.

That same primary study observed sediment expelled from algal-matrix meals, occasional small swimming groups without observed conspecific aggression, and curled, motionless, color-changed resting poses among rocks/corals. Reef-recovery benefits are proposed, not proven. Its field observations do not support an invariably territorial puffer scenario.

**Card — M/P2.** Fresh decision: choose where to aim a short jet, inspect the exposed substrate, then either investigate that spot or switch to a visible feeding option. Four beats: inspect two sand traces associated with existing benthic entities; align the mouth and release a short directed burst; read the small cleared crescent and revealed existing target; approach and handle an eligible meal, or choose an existing sponge patch instead. A visible plume and cleared patch make the action useful immediately. Completion records a successful jet interaction plus the resulting real feeding choice, never an appetite timer. A nearby creature may react through normal behavior but no neighbor is required.

**Mechanic distinction.** s23 already has “blow sand, eat at two sites.” This proposal only earns its own sprint if direction and reveal/inspect/change-choice behavior are implemented; otherwise retain the current puffer journey instead of reskinning s23. There are no geometric nest circles, decoration collection, tool use, or inflation-for-fun requirement. Geometric courtship circles are documented for *Torquigener albomaculosus* in [Mizuuchi et al. 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-30857-0); do not transfer that reproductive role or radial furrows to *A. nigropunctatus*.

**Bounded state/art.** At most two existing target references with reef/id/incarnation, a 0–1 substrate-cover value, aim angle, and a short-lived capped plume pool; no reward spawning or new prey population. Mark existing eligible targets as partially obscured through authored substrate metadata, retaining normal diets and consumption. Hook `approachMeal`, `preparationKind`, the normal consumption event, and shared sand visual utilities; do not globally add worm/shrimp to this puffer's diet. Save cover/identity and phase; freeze while paused/offscreen; reject dead, consumed, regenerated or changed-incarnation targets on resume. Interruption stops the jet without a free bite.

**Acceptance.** A jet from the tail, across a rock, outside range, or into an unrelated cell cannot uncover the target. Mouth-origin direction/range and target overlap must agree with the visible plume. Repeated pulses cannot clone meals. Reveal alone cannot mark feeding complete; normal mouth contact and single consumption must occur. Save/load, pause and reef changes cannot finish the action silently. If nothing eligible is present, show an honest nearby feeding alternative immediately.

**Other sourced opportunities, not additional flagship promises.** Sediment expulsion could enrich a verified algal-matrix bite with a mouth-origin trace, but requires explicit local food metadata because the current diet omits algae; a sediment-clearing chore duplicates Housekeeper/Engineer. Curled rest and color change could enrich ordinary rest after dedicated pose/art work; a second crevice-fitting story duplicates s24. Neither supports instant coral healing, mandatory territorial duels or another required meal checklist.

## s24 — The just-right crevice

**Code audit.** `src/natural-history.js` identifies B. conspicillum. `src/data.js` provides urchin/crab/snail and passive crushing; `src/foraging-journeys.js` shares the shell engine, taking crab then snail. Ordinary `src/shelter.js` uses radius/stillness. New working-tree files `triggerfish-crevice.js`, `triggerfish-crevice-render.js`, unit tests, browser-test source and `assets/story-crevice/README.md` already exist. They author three Coral Cathedral openings, body clearance/occupancy and actual spine contact. At this audit, `reef-stories.js` and `render.js` do not dispatch the draft. Finish and revalidate that work; do not recommend recreating it or claim shipped success from files alone.

**Evidence.** [Waikīkī Aquarium's species account](https://www.waikikiaquarium.org/experience/animal-guide/fishes/triggerfishes/clown-triggerfish/) describes crevice wedging, nighttime rest, a retractable first dorsal spine locked by a smaller spine and maneuverable dorsal/anal-fin swimming. [Two Oceans Aquarium](https://www.aquarium.co.za/animals/clown-triggerfish) independently supports the lock, biting off urchin spines and grunting/clicking. Its nesting-territory warning concerns some triggerfishes, not a verified B. conspicillum breeding protocol. Omit unverified tool use and elaborate breeding claims.

**Species-art gate.** The incumbent s24 atlas painting has dark spots on a pale body, whereas those accounts describe conspicuous white lower-body spots against black and colored lips. Preserve current assets during research, but resolve this concrete mismatch before presenting a species-recognition lesson. An `s24` identifier does not verify the drawing. Any correction must retain gameplay identity and re-author the moving first-spine landmarks.

**Card — M/P1 continuation.** Fresh decision: choose a crevice whose opening and angle fit the body, then deliberately lock or release. Four beats: compare the three openings with full-size body previews; rotate and ease into the fitted one; raise the dorsal spine to its ceiling; release, retract and back out. Completion is the physical enter-lock-release-withdraw sequence. No predator or night wait is required.

**Visible effect.** The fish settles between two rock edges; the dorsal spine rises against the upper edge, with a small outline showing the fit. A wide gap honestly reads “too open to brace here”; a narrow one reads “try another opening.” This is anatomical shelter manipulation, not the current eat-two-things checklist. Avoid a locking bonus that grants blanket invulnerability anywhere in a shelter radius.

**Bounded state/art.** Retain the draft's three anchors, actor identity, selected opening, depth/spine 0–1 and choose/approach/entering/inside/locking/locked/releasing/exiting/complete stages. Finish contextual controls, renderer back/front ordering, save validation and journal reflection using those files. The existing renderer cuts the painted anterior spine from the body and poses that same piece; retain one source removal and one moving layer, with genuine painted rock occlusion. Resolve anatomical/art dependencies rather than drawing a fake second spike. Reduced motion retains the locked physical pose; pause/offscreen cannot advance it. Leave ordinary shelter usable. Local peaceful-story ownership protects required held poses without removing the surrounding reef's predators.

**Lifecycle/acceptance.** Save reef/crevice reference and lock state; on load revalidate actual body placement and species. Pause and offscreen freeze; no offscreen lock, escape or completion. Species/reef change safely releases occupancy. The lock may only engage when body axis and clearance fit inside the selected crevice, never from a proximity button alone; leaving must first visibly retract the spine. Pressing cancel always exits without a failure penalty. Tests should cover correct fit, wrong angle, already occupied opening, interrupted entrance, repeated lock/unlock, and save/load while locked. No NPC needs to become hungry, collide on cue, or arrive from nowhere.

Verify the spine tip touches the same ceiling used by fit checks, all saved phases and legacy imports, local safety release and discovery deduplication through real controls at desktop sizes. A contact sheet or injected state is not playable acceptance.

**Optional extension: A careful urchin bite — M/P3.** The documented spine-biting behavior could support choosing a reachable sector of an existing urchin, taking a mouth-contact preparation bite that visibly removes that sector's spines, then choosing whether to finish the meal or leave it. It earns an extension only with prey-side geometry and lasting visible preparation; another timed shell hold does not. Bound it to one actor/prey identity, at most two prepared sectors and normal single consumption. `foraging-journeys.js`, `feeding-contact.js`, resident motion/rendering and restore validation would need a contact-aware spine mask. Wrong-side/tail/remote contact cannot alter prey; interruption or replacement cannot transfer preparation; no duplicate nutrition or spawned urchin. Keep it optional and peaceful rather than a damage trial.

**Defer acoustic/breeding cards.** Grunts/clicks may provide local sound with a visual equivalent, but the account supplies no decoded meaning or dependable neighbor response. Do not reskin Someone in the Shade as triggerfish language, import titan-triggerfish nesting attacks, or invent tool use and egg care.
