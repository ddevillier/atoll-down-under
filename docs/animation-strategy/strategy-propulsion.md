# Propulsion strategy review — 15 roster animals

Read-only planning review, September 7, 2026. Inspected `animal-profiles.js`, `articulated-motion.js`, `render.js`, `motion.js`, all four actual sprite contact sheets, the owned natural-history entries, and the roster-uplift requirements. No original-game research or implementation performed.

## Assessment

The continuous mesh is a useful foundation: it retains connected painted surfaces, can freeze for reduced motion, and already separates some body plans. It does **not yet constitute convincing species animation**. A common sine field with different frequency/amplitude values cannot carry the requested standard by itself. The present implementation lacks mapped anatomical anchors, separate propulsion and maneuvering controls, a coast state, and anatomy-specific turn handling. Its face assumptions fail especially on long-snouted and coiled animals.

The highest-risk painting is the moray. Its body is already a loop in the source image. `u` is horizontal position, not distance along that curved spine; the current eel formula wiggles the loop as a sheet rather than transmitting a wave from head to tail. The two rays are also not interchangeable: the narrow stingray tail and broad disc need independent regions, while the coffin ray has a very short tail and a rear pelvic region that must not be treated as a stingray whip.

## Concrete engine recommendations

1. **Author landmarks, not only constants.** Add per-sprite eye/head keep-out shape, mouth/contact point, fin roots, caudal base/lobes and a spine path. Coordinates must be measured on the cropped sprite after actual overrides. Every relevant region needs a weight falloff; a global `axis` plus Gaussian at `u=.91` is not a mouth model. The needlefish eye is far behind its jaw tip, and the moray's mouth is above the horizontal midpoint.
2. **Separate travel, propulsion, maneuvering and actions.** Drive stride amplitude from acceleration/effort, not only displacement. Keep a phase accumulator continuous while frequency changes. Cruise → propulsion-off coast → bank/brake → accelerate should be recognizable. Current `motionTime += dt*(.55+activity*.8)` plus frequency and amplitude changes produces activity coupling but no true coast state.
3. **Retire generic whole-head feeding squash for these animals.** `poseFor` still scales the entire creature while feeding, even though the new mesh comments promise no whole-head squash. Use an action envelope at jaw/contact anatomy, with a small root impulse where appropriate. Ray feeding should not mimic a fish lip poke. A creature eating plankton should not pitch the same way as a bottom feeder.
4. **Make turns continuous without pretending a side painting is a full 3-D model.** Current facing interpolation collapses width toward .16 and switches sign at zero. At large shark size this can look like a flat card flipping. Use a short controlled turn state, bank/pitch and trailing-tail offset, restrict front-on compression to a tuned range, and give large fish a curved path. Reduced motion can keep an immediate readable facing change. True frontal poses would require separately reviewed art; do not claim mesh warping creates them.
5. **Use an arc-length spine for the moray.** A simple normalized-x displacement cannot repair the coiled source. Protect the crossing or split body layers around that crossing. If the current painting only supports refuge extension convincingly, record full-swim moray art as an unresolved requirement rather than quietly accepting generic wiggle.
6. **Avoid a mesh render per fish per frame.** Current keys contain unbounded absolute `tick`, phase-offset animals generally miss one another, and each miss creates a canvas and 144–192 clipped source-image draws. A 160-frame cache can thrash in a busy mixed scene. Precompute or lazily reuse cyclic phase bins by profile/size/effort/action; separate inexpensive root transform from expensive deformation. Quantize to a few size tiers and bounded periodic phase. Measure real scenes, including a fast pan/reef switch that cold-starts caches; memory cap alone does not prove performance.
7. **Keep group policy species-specific.** s0/s42/s49/s55 have explicit schooling support in existing prose. The available guide facts are weaker for s17/s28: research only those specific claims if asserting natural schooling. Schools need preferred spacing scaled to actual body width, acceleration limits, obstacle handling, and independently delayed responses. Hunting needs reservations or staggered choices so all school members do not attack a single target at once.

## Acceptance evidence before marking these animals done

- For all 15, review an actual-size motion strip/video of cruise, slow/coast, reversal, meal action, rest/departure and disturbance interruption; include both facing directions. A profile table or frozen screenshot is insufficient.
- Review each at gameplay size and large inspection size. Eyes, painted stripes and gill position stay coherent; no open mesh seams, detached fin tips, stretched snouts, clipped tails, or breathing belly that moves the entire skull.
- At least the sprat, fusilier and mackerel groups demonstrate a shared turn while retaining personal space and independent phases. Show a feeding interruption and rejoin through ordinary gameplay, with no added population.
- Compare the four moving sharks in one clip with labels hidden: white shark travel, blacktip circuit, tiger burst/coast, and whitetip refuge transition should be distinguishable. Wobbegong should visibly belong to the seabed routine.
- Ray and coffin-ray clips must show different tail/disc treatment. Moray must pass a spine/overlap review at maximum deformation. If it fails, an alternate local asset is a real dependency, not a test to relax.
- Pause freezes all deformation and interpolation; resume has no phase jump. Reduced motion preserves position/action cues with no continuous fin/mesh motion. Travel/death/respawn clears stale pose and target state.
- Measure steady and cold-cache frame times in ordinary 1600×900 and 1024×768 scenes with representative populations; record frame-time distribution and allocated cache bytes, not only an average FPS or unit-test timing. Test new routines without unlimited hunger or predator aggressiveness masking their resting states.
