# Reef roster animation strategy — read-only review

Reviewed 7 September 2026: `src/animal-profiles.js`, `src/articulated-motion.js`, `src/render.js`, `src/natural-history.js`, all four sprite sheets, and the current standalone overrides for goby, butterflyfish, squirrelfish, puffer, coral trout and batfish. This covers the 39 reef-fish IDs assigned to this review. No runtime files were changed.

## Main recommendation

The continuous mesh is a useful joined-surface foundation, but **a per-species row of amplitude and frequency values does not yet create per-species articulation**. Add explicit anatomical attachment regions and behavioral pose transitions. Prioritize visible pectoral propulsion, independently trailing long fins, contact-aware feeding and recognizable rest poses. Use the existing sprite identity while acknowledging that several paintings do not match the field-guide identity closely enough to support exact anatomy claims.

All timing, bend amounts, paths and animation signatures below are authored visual design. Biological grounding is limited to the independently sourced text already in `natural-history.js`; neither these timings nor the mesh are measured animal kinematics. No original-game comparison was undertaken.

## Specific shortcomings of the current mesh

1. **Pectorals are mostly excluded.** The generic fin field acts near the upper/lower outline (`abs(v-axis) > .14`). The obvious pectoral fin painted in the middle of the goby, butterflyfish, puffer and batfish consequently receives little independent motion. A hover fish whose main visible stabilizing fin is frozen will still look like a floating picture.
2. **Outline is not anatomy.** The same edge field can bend a cowfish horn, a damselfish's flank, a rigid dorsal spine and a soft fin. `rigid` reduces the tail drive but does not create a rigid body mask. The comment claiming protected eyes/bodies is stronger than the current local fields guarantee.
3. **Thin appendages need their own geometry.** Twelve columns and six rows cannot meaningfully articulate a long narrow Moorish-idol/bannerfish streamer, goatfish barbels, frogfish lure or lionfish individual rays. Shared lattice vertices couple unrelated shapes through transparent regions.
4. **The generic mouth is in the wrong place for unusual silhouettes.** A ventral bell at u=.91 and axis+.09 does not describe every mouth; butterflyfish/trumpetfish snouts and the frogfish's near-vertical gape need actual jaw landmarks. The current feed parameter begins at consumption, so a snap can occur after prey has disappeared. Show anticipation before the consumption event, contact at the event, recovery after it.
5. **Turns still flatten to a narrow sheet.** The current facing interpolation bottoms out at .16. It is preferable to instant mirroring, but tall batfish and fan-like lionfish collapse conspicuously. A brief banking pose, path curvature and partial fin overlap can reduce the effect without inventing a full 3D back view.
6. **Activity currently mainly changes speed.** The mesh receives feed/retract and movement effort, but not resting, grazing, braking, striking, cleaning-client pose or turn effort. These must become explicit semantic render inputs, without deriving state from English status strings.
7. **Frame caching needs measurement at ordinary fish density.** Absolute 24 Hz ticks plus individual phase offsets, effort quantization and feeding buckets can produce many unique cached canvases. A 160-entry cache is not evidence that dozens of species animate affordably. Use a repeating cycle phase key where motion repeats, shared neutral frames per species/size and lower distant-NPC update rates. Preserve complete action phase for one-shot transitions.

## Shared engineering that preserves species identity

Use a small authored rig schema per sprite: rigid head/body polygons; eye protect disk; mouth point and lower-jaw hinge; tail peduncle/root; pectoral root and polygon; dorsal/anal soft-fin polygons; rigid spine exclusions; optional appendage paths with a pinned root and 3–5 control points. Coordinates must be recorded against **the renderer's extracted/cropped sprite**, not the source PNG bounding box: some overrides contain broad transparent antialiased halos. The locations named in the roster table below are the landmarks to author; visual estimates from a contact sheet must not be silently treated as tested coordinates.

Deform each anatomy region with a smooth attachment falloff. Preserve the eye as a rigid island carried by head pose. Let pectorals rotate/shear around their painted root, with a small amplitude at rest and higher amplitude during braking. Soft dorsal/anal fin rays may ripple; stiff spines should fan by rotation at the root rather than bend like cloth. Keep rigid body masks independent of the fin region. A stable jaw silhouette is more valuable than a universal whole-head squash.

For long appendages, move a narrow mesh around a traced centerline with lag driven by turn/acceleration plus a gentle authored water oscillation. Do not draw a second duplicate streamer over the painted one. If separate masked pieces are used, the original appendage must be excluded from the underlying body draw and its attachment seam tested at both facing directions.

Transitions should have at least approach/brake, contact/strike, recover, and settle/depart states. Author readable durations around 150–350 ms for ordinary bites and 500–1000 ms for settling; these are gameplay values, not biological assertions. Preserve immediate steering responsiveness: an action animation should not trap the player. At normal game size, movement silhouette and trajectory carry the read; detailed gill/eye/jaw motion is a close-up bonus.

## Verification standard

- Make an animation review scene with the 39 actual extracted sprites. Show idle, cruise, accelerate, brake, turn, feed and authored rest side by side; long-appendage and rigid-body exclusions need slow-motion inspection. Include both facing directions.
- For each species record the visible signature and any outstanding art limitation. Passing a family math test is not equivalent to visually approving each species.
- Test at ordinary in-game width first, then large close-up. A gill that only moves at 400 px does not satisfy a readable gameplay uplift for an 80 px fish.
- Include predator/food interruptions of rest; a settling animation must not suppress escape or trap steering. Verify school/pair spacing, simultaneous refuge arrivals and cleaning transitions without new animal density.
- Paused poses freeze; reduced motion suppresses loops and long trailing sway but retains static activity cues. Narration, saves, action input and animal counts stay independent of visual timing.
- Profile a real populated reef and the title with cache hit/miss and frame times, including a mixed roster and feeding events. Explicitly measure before claiming whole-game performance.

## Suggested implementation order

First make anatomy masks and semantic pose inputs work on s2, s23, s4, s20, s41 and s59: these expose the central-fin, rigid-horn, streamer, fan, rod-body and bottom-contact problems. Then author the remaining 33 rigs using shared engines with distinct anchors. Finish by reviewing every species and investigating species that still look like sliding paintings. These representative prototypes are engineering steps, not the definition of completion.
